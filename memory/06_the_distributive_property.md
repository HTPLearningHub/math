This is the first chapter about **multiplication**. Chapters 1 to 4 were parts of a whole,
Chapter 5 was whole-number addition and subtraction. Chapter 6 defines multiplication itself
(§ 1.1) because nothing before it did, then builds the distributive property on top.

## Formulas and facts that live here

* $5 \times 3$ means $3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15$ (§ 1.1)
* $a + b = b + a$ and $a \times b = b \times a$ (§ 1.3)
* $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$ and $(a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c)$ (§ 1.4)
* Counter-examples (§ 1.5): $7 - 3 = 4$ but $3 - 7$ is not; $10 \div 2 = 5$ but
  $2 \div 10 = 0.2$; $(10 - 4) - 3 = 3$ but $10 - (4 - 3) = 9$; $(12 \div 6) \div 2 = 1$ but
  $12 \div (6 \div 2) = 4$
* $a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$ (§ 2.4)
* $a \times (b - c) = (a \times b) - (a \times c)$ (§ 4.1)
* $(b + c) \times a = (b \times a) + (c \times a)$ (§ 4.3)
* $a \times (b + c + d) = (a \times b) + (a \times c) + (a \times d)$ (§ 4.2)
* area of a rectangle $=$ height $\times$ width (§ 3.1)
* Worked numbers: $5 \times (3 + 4) = 35$ (§ 2.2-2.3); $4 \times (6 + 2) = 32$ (§ 2.5);
  $5 \times (30 + 4) = 170$ (§ 3.2); $3 \times (4 + 5) = 27$, wrong version $17$, gap $10$
  (§ 3.4); $5 \times (10 - 2) = 40$ (§ 4.1); $2 \times (4 + 3 + 1) = 16$ (§ 4.2);
  $(6 + 2) \times 4 = 32$ (§ 4.3); $5 \times 17 = 85$ (§ 5.2); $6 \times 345 = 2070$ (§ 5.3);
  $7 \times 98 = 686$ (§ 5.4)
* Questions (§ 7): $5 \times (6 + 3) = 45$, $6 \times (10 - 4) = 36$, $8 \times 21 = 168$,
  $9 \times 49 = 441$, $4 \times (50 + 8 + 3) = 244$, $7 \times 198 = 1386$,
  $2 + (3 \times 4) = 14$ against $(2 + 3) \times (2 + 4) = 30$, $6 \times 99 = 594$
* Every number above was checked with Python before it was written down.

Three layout lessons worth keeping:

* A hatched block cannot carry a label directly. In fig_04 and fig_05 the label sits in a
  white rounded `bbox` on top of the hatch; without it the letters are unreadable. The hatch
  colour also has to be set through the patch's `edgecolor` (with `linewidth=0`), otherwise
  matplotlib draws the hatch in black, which is far too heavy.
* Figures 3, 4 and 5 fix the figure height from the data range
  (`H = W * (Y_HI - Y_LO) / (X_HI - X_LO)`) so that `set_aspect("equal")` never leaves a band
  of empty page. Change one limit and the height follows automatically.
* fig_06 is deliberately **not** an area model. $300$, $40$ and $5$ cannot be drawn side by
  side to scale - the $5$ would be thinner than its own outline - so it is a flow diagram, and
  the script says why.

## Additions made because the source uses them without explaining them

* **Multiplication itself.** The source lists it under "prerequisite knowledge" in one line.
  Nothing earlier in the book defines it, so § 1.1 does: repeated addition, then *factor* and
  *product*.
* **Area.** The source draws an area model but never says what area is. § 3.1 defines it as
  counting equal squares and derives height $\times$ width from § 1.1, so the model explains
  something instead of being a second thing to accept.
* **Why the picture is a proof.** The source presents the area model as a helpful picture.
  § 3.3 turns it into the argument: a cut cannot create or destroy squares, and the same
  sentence works for any $a$, $b$ and $c$.
* **Why the multiplier may stand on the right.** The source states the right-side form and
  says "because multiplication is commutative". § 4.3 spells the step out: it is the same
  product with its factors swapped, so it is not a new rule.
* **How to choose a split.** The source shows three splits but never says how to pick one.
  § 5.5 gives three questions (just below a round number, just above, neither) and the warning
  to check that the pieces add back up.
* **The dot as a multiplication sign.** The source writes $5 \cdot 3$ throughout. The book
  writes $\times$ everywhere, to match chapters 1 to 5 and to keep the dot away from the
  decimal point; § 1.1 has a one-line note so the reader can read other sources.

## Corrections and simplifications made to the source

* **The ASCII area model was replaced.** The source draws its rectangle with `+`, `-` and `|`
  characters. That became fig_03, drawn to scale with countable unit squares.
* **Brackets were kept on the right-hand side of every rule.** The source writes
  $a \cdot b + a \cdot c$, which is only unambiguous if you already know that multiplication
  is done before addition - a rule the book has not taught. The book writes
  $(a \times b) + (a \times c)$ and says why in a note (§ 2.4). **If a later transcript
  teaches the full order of operations, that is the place to relax this.**
* **"For any real numbers" was dropped.** The source states the rules "for any real numbers
  $a$, $b$, $c$". Real numbers are not in the book, and negative numbers are explicitly out of
  scope (Ch. 5 § 6.1), so the book says "for any three numbers".
* **The source's Mistake 2 was re-explained.** It says the distributive property "does not
  apply when adding a number to a multiplication group", which describes the symptom. § 7
  Question 8 and § 8 give the reason: the operation outside the bracket is an addition, so
  there is nothing to hand out.
* **The source's own worked examples were folded into the teaching sections** rather than
  collected in a separate "examples" chapter, to avoid explaining each method twice.
  $4 \times (6 + 2)$ is § 2.5, $7 \times 98$ is § 5.4, $6 \times 345$ is § 5.3.
* **The commutative and associative properties were given pictures and counter-examples.**
  The source lists them as bare formulas. § 1.3 has fig_01 and § 1.5 has four worked
  counter-examples, because "subtraction does not obey these" is the part beginners forget.

## Not covered yet - waiting for a source

* **The full order of operations** (PEMDAS / BODMAS). The source teaches only "brackets
  first", so § 1.2 teaches only that. Until a transcript covers it, every rule in Chapter 6
  keeps its brackets.
* **Long multiplication in columns.** This chapter multiplies by splitting; the standard
  column algorithm for two multi-digit numbers is still missing from the book.
* **Two brackets multiplied together**, $(a + b) \times (c + d)$, and therefore FOIL. The
  source never mentions it.
* **Factoring - using the rule backwards to pull a common factor out**, as in
  $15 + 20 = 5 \times (3 + 4)$. § 5.1 reads the rule right to left for mental arithmetic, but
  the word *factoring* and the algebraic use are not taught.
* **Algebra with letters as unknowns.** The source mentions $x$ and $y$ as motivation only;
  § 8 keeps that to two sentences.
* **Division and its own distribution**, and long division as an algorithm - still open from
  Chapter 5.
* **Negative numbers**, so distributing a minus sign over a bracket, as in
  $-(b + c)$ or $a \times (b - c)$ where $c$ is larger than $b$, is out of scope.
* Multiplication and division of decimals and fractions. Ch. 4 § 6.1 multiplies a decimal by a
  whole number; nothing multiplies two decimals or two fractions.
