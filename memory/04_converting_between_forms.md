## Terms defined here (do not define them again anywhere else)

equivalent, unit fraction, terminating decimal, repeating decimal, round (rounding),
sales tax.

Also introduced here: the bar notation $0.\overline{3}$, and the arrow-chain notation
$\xrightarrow{\ \text{divide}\ }$.

## Formulas and facts that live here

* $\frac{a}{b} = a \div b$ used forwards: divide the top by the bottom to get the decimal (§ 2.1)
* Unit fractions: $\frac{1}{2} = 0.5$, $\frac{1}{4} = 0.25$, $\frac{1}{5} = 0.2$,
  $\frac{1}{8} = 0.125$, $\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$, $\frac{1}{20} = 0.05$ (§ 2.2)
* $\frac{3}{5} = 0.6 = 60\%$; $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$ (§ 2.2, § 2.5)
* $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375 = 37.5\%$ - the fraction Ch. 3 could not reach (§ 2.3, § 2.5)
* $\frac{9}{20} = 0.45 = 45\%$ (§ 2.3, § 8 Q2); $\frac{5}{8} = 0.625 = 62.5\%$ (§ 5.3)
* $\frac{1}{3} = 0.\overline{3} = 33.\overline{3}\%$; $\frac{2}{3} = 0.\overline{6} = 66.\overline{6}\%$ (§ 2.4, § 4.2)
* Decimal to fraction: digits over $10$ / $100$ / $1000$, then simplify.
  $0.7 = \frac{7}{10}$, $0.65 = \frac{13}{20}$, $0.065 = \frac{13}{200}$,
  $0.375 = \frac{375}{1000} = \frac{3}{8}$ (§ 3)
* The benchmark table of thirteen rows (§ 4.2)
* Order: $\frac{21}{50} < 0.43 < 45\%$ (§ 5.2) and
  $\frac{3}{5} < 61.5\% < 0.62 < \frac{5}{8}$ (§ 5.3)
* Decimal times whole number: multiply without the point, then give the answer the same
  number of decimal places. $32 \times 0.15 = 4.80$; $50 \times 0.0825 = 4.125$;
  $120 \times 0.375 = 45$ (§ 6.1, § 6.2, § 6.4)
* $8.25\% = 0.0825$; tax on $50$ is $4.125$, which rounds to $4.13$; total $54.13$ (§ 6.2, § 6.3)
* Rounding rule: look at the first digit you drop; $5$ or more rounds up (§ 6.3)
* $6.5\% = 0.065 = \frac{13}{200}$ (§ 8 Q4); $15\%$ of $32$ is $4.80$ (§ 8 Q6)

* Rule 1 (percent to fraction), Rule 2 (percent to decimal), Rule 3 (decimal to percent) and

* The "cross out one zero from each" tip - this is Ch. 1 § 3.2 with $c = 10$, so it is used

* Discounts and tips as ideas - Ch. 3 § 6 and § 7. Chapter 4 only adds what is new: a

## Additions made because the source uses them without explaining them

* **Long division into decimal places.** The source says "Method B (Direct Long Division):
  Divide numerator by denominator" and nothing more. The book teaches it as a share-and-trade
  table: share, then cut every leftover into ten smaller pieces and share again. The table
  has no traditional long-division bracket, because that layout is text art.
* **Multiplying a decimal by a whole number.** The source computes $50 \times 0.0825$,
  $32 \times 0.15$ and $120 \times 0.375$, but the book had only taught decimal *addition*
  (Ch. 2 § 6). § 6.1 teaches it as counting pieces, then states the decimal-places rule.
* **Rounding.** The source says $4.125$ "rounds to $4.13$ in financial transactions" without
  a rule. § 6.3 gives the rule and the warning to round only at the end.
* **Repeating decimals and the bar notation.** The source puts $0.333\dots$ and $33.\bar{3}\%$
  in its benchmark table with no explanation. § 2.4 explains *why* the division loops.

## Corrections made to the source

* The source's "Conversion Navigator" was ASCII art, and its arrows between FRACTION and
  DECIMAL were drawn in only one direction each with the labels sitting under the wrong
  boxes. It was redrawn as fig_04 with all six arrows.
* The source's Rule 5 (decimal to fraction) never mentions counting the zeros, and then its
  own Solution 4 relies on it ($0.065 = \frac{65}{1000}$). § 3.1 makes the rule explicit:
  count **all** the places after the point, including zeros.
* The source's Example 2 gives the final answer as "$4.125$ (or $4.13$)", which leaves the
  reader unsure which one to write. The book computes $4.125$, then rounds once, and says
  that the amount handed over is $4.13$.
* The source's Example 5 writes "$45\% = \frac{45}{100} = 45\%$", a step that does nothing.
  The book simply says $45\%$ is already a percent and leaves it alone.
* The source's benchmark table lists $\frac{1}{1}$ as a fraction. It is kept, because it
  usefully anchors $100\%$, but the chapter never calls it a *proper* fraction (Ch. 1 § 2.4).
