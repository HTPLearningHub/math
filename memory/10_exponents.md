# Chapter 10 - Exponents

File: [10_Exponents/10_Exponents.md](./../10_Exponents/10_Exponents.md)

Folder name and title match. Both are fixed - do not rename either.

This is the fifth operation, and it is placed here because it needs almost everything before
it: Ch. 6 for repeated addition and the rectangle, Ch. 7 for column multiplication, Ch. 1 for
cancelling, and Ch. 9 for the negative exponents in § 7 and § 9.

The chapter is built on one move, used five times: **write the power out in full, count what
happened, and name that**. § 4 counts two boxes together, § 5 counts what survives cancelling,
§ 8 counts a rectangle. § 6 and § 7 cannot be counted that way, so they are settled twice over -
once by the halving ladder (fig_07) and once by a proof from Rule 2.

## Terms defined here (do not define them again anywhere else)

base, exponent, power, exponentiation, squaring, cubing, caret, exponential growth, reciprocal.

**Multiplication**, **factor**, **product**, **brackets** and the **commutative** and
**associative** properties are Ch. 6 § 1. **Simplifying a fraction** is Ch. 1 § 3.3, and § 5.1
links rather than re-explaining it. **A fraction bar is a division** is Ch. 1 § 2.2. **Area of a
rectangle** is Ch. 6 § 3.1. **Negative number** and **sign** are Ch. 9 § 1.3, and **adding a
negative** and **subtracting a negative** are Ch. 9 § 4.3 and § 4.4. The glossary ends with a
Note listing all of these with links.

## Formulas and facts that live here

* $x^{a} = x \times x \times \dots \times x$, with $a$ of them (§ 2.1)
* $x^{1} = x$ (§ 2.4) - needed so that a plain number can join a rule
* $x^{a} \times x^{b} = x^{a+b}$ (§ 4.2)
* $\frac{x^{a}}{x^{b}} = x^{a-b}$, $x \neq 0$ (§ 5.2)
* $x^{0} = 1$, $x \neq 0$ (§ 6.4)
* $x^{-a} = \frac{1}{x^{a}}$, $x \neq 0$ (§ 7.4)
* $(x^{a})^{b} = x^{a \times b}$ (§ 8.2)
* An exponent on a bracket reaches every factor inside it (§ 8.4) - **not one of the five
  rules in the source, which uses it anyway; see the additions below**
* $x^{a} + x^{b}$ cannot be combined at all (§ 9.2)
* If $x^{m} = x^{n}$ then $m = n$ (§ 9.5) - stated only for a base of $2$, in words
* The § 6.3 proof, which is the chapter's cleanest piece of reasoning: Rule 2 says
  $\frac{x^{3}}{x^{3}} = x^{0}$, ordinary division says the same fraction is $1$, so $x^{0} = 1$.
  § 7.3 repeats the shape on $\frac{x^{5}}{x^{7}}$ to reach $x^{-2} = \frac{1}{x^{2}}$
* Worked numbers, all checked with Python before they were written down:
  $3^{4} = 81$ (via $9$, $27$); $6^{5} = 7\,776$ (via $36$, $216$, $1\,296$);
  $5^{2} = 25$; $5^{3} = 125$; $2^{2} \times 2^{3} = 32$; $\frac{2^{5}}{2^{3}} = 4$;
  $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$; $2^{-2} = \frac{1}{4}$; $(2^{3})^{2} = 64$ and $2^{6} = 64$;
  $(2x^{3}y^{2})^{3} = 8x^{9}y^{6}$; $\frac{(2x^{3}y^{2})^{3}}{4x^{4}y^{8}} = \frac{2x^{5}}{y^{2}}$;
  $2^{3} + 2^{2} = 12$; $2^{n} \times 2^{3} = 32 \Rightarrow n = 2$
* The § 3.2 growth table: $n = 0,1,2,3,4,5,10$ against $2+n$, $2 \times n$, $2^{n}$, ending
  $12$, $20$, $1\,024$
* The chessboard counts: $2^{7} = 128$, $2^{15} = 32\,768$, $2^{23} = 8\,388\,608$,
  $2^{31} = 2\,147\,483\,648$, $2^{63} = 9\,223\,372\,036\,854\,775\,808$ (nineteen digits).
  Square $n$ carries $2^{n-1}$
* Questions (§ 11): $4^{3} = 64$; $a^{5} \times a^{2} = a^{7}$; $7^{0} = 1$;
  $\frac{k^{9}}{k^{4}} = k^{5}$; $3^{-4} = \frac{1}{81}$; $(p^{2})^{5} \times p^{-3} = p^{7}$;
  $\frac{(3a^{2}b^{4})^{2}}{9a^{-1}b^{5}} = a^{5}b^{3}$ (the $a$ part is $a^{4-(-1)} = a^{5}$);
  $2^{n} \times 2^{3} = 32 \Rightarrow n = 2$; Q9 is the $2^{3} + 2^{2} = 32$ mistake, answer
  $12$; Q10 compares $(2^{4})^{3} = 2^{12}$ with $2^{4} \times 2^{3} = 2^{7}$ without evaluating

Colour convention, carried on from Chapter 9, with **one new meaning added here**:

Layout lessons worth keeping, on top of the Chapter 9 list:

* **Mathtext cannot colour one digit inside a string.** fig_01 and fig_06 were both first
  written by drawing the expression in black and then painting the key digits over the top; the
  black edges show through at print size. Both now draw each digit once, in its final colour.
* **The Chapter 9 warning about a dashed rule cutting through a neighbour happened again**, in
  fig_07, where the split line ran straight through a "half it" arrow. It stops at $x = 5.75$
  now, short of the arrow column at $6.05$.
* **A shaded panel needs clearance from the closing sentence.** fig_07's panel first ended
  almost on top of it; the figure was made taller and the row spacing tightened.
* **A strike-through line drawn across a stacked fraction lands in the middle of it.** fig_08
  first struck out its two wrong answers that way and neither could be read. They carry a drawn
  cross beside them instead.
* **An annotation arrow reaching across a chart will cross the curve.** fig_03's labels for
  $2+n$ and $2 \times n$ at $n = 10$ had to cut through the $2^{n}$ curve to get there, so those
  two values are listed in a corner block instead.
* **Plain words and a formula at the same `fontsize` do not look the same weight.** fig_10 sets
  17 for a text result and 22 for a mathtext one.

## Additions made because the source uses them without explaining them

* **The power-of-a-product rule** (§ 8.4). The source's hardest example and its hardest
  practice problem both open a bracket like $(2x^{3}y^{2})^{3}$, and it calls this "the Power
  Rule for each factor" - a sixth rule it never states. § 8.4 derives it from Ch. 6 § 1.3 and
  says plainly that the plain number inside the bracket gets the exponent too.
* **Implied multiplication** (§ 8.4, Note). The source writes $2x^{3}y^{2}$ from its Level 3
  example onwards and never says that the spaces mean $\times$.
* **What a letter in a rule stands for** (§ 2.5). Ch. 6 § 8 mentions it in a closing paragraph;
  this is the first chapter where the reader must *use* letters, so it is said properly once.
* **$x^{1} = x$** (§ 2.4). The source lists $2^{1} = 2$ inside a doubling list and never draws
  the general conclusion, which § 4 then needs.
* **The word *power* for the whole expression** (§ 2.1). The source's own table calls the
  exponent "Exponent (Power)", which leaves no word for $5^{3}$ itself. The book splits the two
  and adds a Note that other books do not.
* **Why $x \neq 0$** (§ 5.2, Note). The source prints the condition on three rules and never
  explains it. One Note covers all three.
* **The third job of the minus sign** (§ 7.5). Ch. 9 § 4.2 gave the minus sign two jobs. A
  negative exponent is a third, and saying so is what stops the $2^{-3} = -8$ mistake.
* **The halving ladder** (§ 6.2, § 7.2, fig_07). The source proves $x^{0}$ and $x^{-a}$ only
  from the quotient rule, which is correct but gives the reader nothing to picture. The ladder
  is added as the intuition and the proofs are kept as the proofs.
* **Q10** (§ 11). The source has no question that asks the reader to *choose* between Rule 1 and
  Rule 5 without computing, which is the skill § 9.1 is teaching.

## Corrections and simplifications made to the source

* **The source contradicts itself about which number is repeated.** Its introduction reads
  $2 \times 3$ as $2 + 2 + 2$; its prerequisite section reads $3 \times 4$ as $4 + 4 + 4$. The
  book follows Ch. 6 § 1.1 throughout: the first number is the count. § 1.2 uses
  $3 \times 2 = 2 + 2 + 2$.
* **The rice claim was dropped.** The source says the reward would cover India in a layer one
  metre deep. It would not - the real figure is closer to a sixth of that. § 3.3 keeps only the
  claim that survives checking: it is more rice than the world grows in hundreds of years.
* **Scientific notation was avoided.** The source writes $2^{63} \approx 9.22 \times 10^{18}$,
  a notation the book has never taught. § 3.3 prints the number in full and says it has
  nineteen digits.
* **The dot for multiplication was replaced by $\times$**, as Ch. 6 § 1.1 requires.
* **Both ASCII diagrams were dropped.** No ASCII art in the book (CLAUDE.md § 7). The anatomy
  diagram became fig_02 and the selection flowchart became fig_10.
* **The flowchart was rebuilt around shapes, not questions.** The source's version asks "same
  base?" and then branches on the operation, which leaves "power of a power" hanging off a
  question that does not apply to it, and leaves addition out entirely. fig_10 has one cell per
  shape, with addition as the fourth cell, in red.
* **The source's separate "worked examples" section was dissolved**, as in Chapters 7, 8 and 9.
  Each example now sits in the section that needs it: $3^{4}$ and $6^{5}$ in § 2.3,
  $y^{3} \times y^{4}$ in § 4.3, $\frac{m^{7}}{m^{2}}$ in § 5.3, $2^{-3}$ in § 7.4,
  $(a^{3})^{4} \times a^{-5}$ in § 9.3 and the long one in § 9.4.
* **The "common mistakes" were used twice.** Each is placed as a Warning at the point where the
  reader could make it (§ 1.3, § 4.4, § 6.4, § 7.5, § 8.3, § 9.2) and then collected again in
  § 12. The addition mistake also became question Q9.
* **The source's own "Multiplication" column contains $2 \times 0 = 0$ at step 0** while its
  addition column starts at $2$. That is correct but looks like an error, so § 3.2 keeps the
  table and lets § 3.1 establish first that the comparison is about the *shape* of the growth.

## Not covered yet - waiting for a source

* **The order of operations** (PEMDAS / BODMAS). Still open from Chapters 6, 7, 8 and 9, and now
  more visible than ever: § 9.4 has to open a bracket before anything else, and the chapter says
  so in § 12 without giving the general rule. This is the most obvious hole in the book.
* **Fractional exponents and roots**, including square roots. Nothing in the book mentions them,
  and the source does not either.
* **A negative base**, such as $(-2)^{3}$ or $(-2)^{4}$, and the odd/even pattern. Every base in
  this chapter is positive; only the *exponent* is ever negative.
* **$0^{0}$**, and what happens to $0$ raised to anything. § 5.2's Note only says the base must
  not be zero.
* **Scientific notation**, deliberately avoided in § 3.3. It is the obvious next use of powers
  of ten and the book has now laid every piece of groundwork for it.
* **The power of a quotient**, $\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{a}$. § 8.4 covers a product inside a
  bracket only.
* **Powers of a decimal or a fraction**, such as $(0.5)^{3}$ or $\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2}$.
  Every base worked out here is a whole number.
* **Why $a^{m} = a^{n}$ forces $m = n$** in general. § 9.5 argues it for a base of $2$, in
  words, and does not claim more.
* **Logarithms** - the question "what exponent gives this number", which § 9.5 asks and answers
  only by doubling until it lands.
* **Division by zero**, still untouched, as in Chapters 8 and 9. § 5.2 leans on it.
* **Estimation**, still open from Chapters 5, 7, 8 and 9.
