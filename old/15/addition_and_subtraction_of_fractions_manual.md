# Addition and Subtraction of Fractions: A Complete Beginner's Guide

---

## 1. Introduction

Fractions are everywhere in our daily lives. Whether you are slicing a pizza, measuring ingredients for a recipe, or calculating time, you use fractions constantly. 

A **fraction** represents a part of a whole quantity. Adding and subtracting fractions allows us to combine parts together or find out how much of a part remains.

While adding and subtracting whole numbers like $2 + 3 = 5$ is simple, fractions require one extra rule: **the parts must be the exact same size before you can combine them**. 

In this tutorial, you will learn step-by-step how to add and subtract fractions—both when they have the same denominator and when they have different denominators. By the end of this guide, you will be able to solve these problems with confidence!

---

## 2. Prerequisite Knowledge

Before diving into adding and subtracting fractions, let us review a few basic mathematical ideas that form our foundation.

### A. What is a Fraction?
A fraction is written as two numbers stacked on top of each other, separated by a horizontal line:
$$\frac{a}{b}$$

* **Top number ($a$)**: Tells us how many parts we have.
* **Bottom number ($b$)**: Tells us how many equal parts make up one whole unit. Note that $b$ can never equal zero ($b \neq 0$).

---

### B. Least Common Multiple (LCM)
The **Least Common Multiple (LCM)** of two numbers is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of both numbers.

* **Example**: Find the LCM of $3$ and $4$.
  * Multiples of $3$: $3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, \dots$
  * Multiples of $4$: $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, \dots$
  * Common multiples: $12, 24, \dots$
  * Smallest common multiple: **$12$**.
  * Therefore, $\text{LCM}(3, 4) = 12$.

---

### C. Equivalent Fractions
**Equivalent fractions** are different fractions that represent the exact same amount or value.

You can create an equivalent fraction by multiplying (or dividing) both the top number and the bottom number by the same non-zero number. This works because multiplying by $\frac{n}{n}$ is the same as multiplying by $1$, which does not change the actual value of the quantity.

* **Example**:
  $$\frac{1}{3} = \frac{1 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \frac{4}{12}$$
  Here, $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{4}{12}$ describe the exact same portion of a whole.

---

### D. Simplifying (Reducing) Fractions
Simplifying a fraction means rewriting it using the smallest possible whole numbers for the top and bottom. To simplify a fraction, divide both the top and bottom numbers by their **Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)**.

* **Example**: Simplify $\frac{4}{8}$.
  * Both $4$ and $8$ can be divided by $4$:
  $$\frac{4 \div 4}{8 \div 4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

---

## 3. Important Definitions

Here are the essential mathematical terms you need to know for this topic:

* **Numerator**: The top number of a fraction. In $\frac{1}{8}$, the numerator is $1$.
* **Denominator**: The bottom number of a fraction. In $\frac{1}{8}$, the denominator is $8$. It reports the size or magnitude of each slice.
* **Like Fractions**: Fractions that have the **same denominator**. For example, $\frac{1}{8}$ and $\frac{3}{8}$ are like fractions.
* **Unlike Fractions**: Fractions that have **different denominators**. For example, $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{4}$ are unlike fractions.
* **Least Common Denominator (LCD)**: The Least Common Multiple of the denominators of two or more fractions. For $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{4}$, the LCD is $12$.

---

## 4. Main Concepts

---

### Concept 1: Adding and Subtracting Fractions with the Same Denominator

When fractions have the same denominator, their pieces are already the exact same size. 

Because the size of the piece (the denominator) does not change when you combine them, **you only add or subtract the numerators (the top numbers) and keep the denominator the same**.

Think of it like counting items:
$$1\text{ apple} + 3\text{ apples} = 4\text{ apples}$$

In the same way:
$$1\text{ eighth} + 3\text{ eighths} = 4\text{ eighths} \implies \frac{1}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{4}{8}$$

> **Important Note**: Never add or subtract the denominators! The denominator simply reports what kind of slice you have.

---

### Concept 2: Adding and Subtracting Fractions with Different Denominators

When fractions have different denominators, you cannot add or subtract them directly because their pieces are different sizes. 

For example, $1$ slice out of a $3$-slice pizza is larger than $1$ slice out of a $4$-slice pizza. Combining them directly would be like trying to add $1$ apple and $3$ oranges without converting them to a common category like "fruits".

To solve this, follow these three steps:
1. **Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD)** of the fractions.
2. **Convert each fraction** into an equivalent fraction that has the LCD as its bottom number.
3. **Add or subtract the numerators**, keeping the common denominator unchanged.

---

### Concept 3: Simplifying the Final Answer

After performing addition or subtraction, always check if your final fraction can be simplified. If the numerator and denominator share a common factor greater than $1$, divide both by that factor.

---

### Concept 4: Negative Results in Subtraction

When you subtract a larger fraction from a smaller fraction, the result will be negative. The rule remains identical: subtract the numerators over the common denominator.

---

## 5. Formulas and Rules

### Rule 1: Addition of Like Fractions
$$\frac{a}{c} + \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a + b}{c} \quad \text{where } c \neq 0$$

### Rule 2: Subtraction of Like Fractions
$$\frac{a}{c} - \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a - b}{c} \quad \text{where } c \neq 0$$

### Rule 3: General Formula for Unlike Fractions
To compute $\frac{a}{b} \pm \frac{c}{d}$:

1. Identify $L = \text{LCM}(b, d)$.
2. Rewrite each fraction using $L$:
   $$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \times (L / b)}{L}, \quad \frac{c}{d} = \frac{c \times (L / d)}{L}$$
3. Combine:
   $$\frac{a}{b} \pm \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times (L / b) \pm c \times (L / d)}{L}$$

## 6. Step-by-Step Examples

---

### Example 1 (Easy): Same Denominators

**Problem**: Calculate $\frac{1}{8} + \frac{3}{8}$.

* **Step 1: Check the denominators.**
  Both fractions have a denominator of $8$. They are like fractions.

* **Step 2: Add the numerators.**
  $$1 + 3 = 4$$

* **Step 3: Write the sum over the common denominator.**
  $$\frac{1}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{1 + 3}{8} = \frac{4}{8}$$

* **Step 4: Simplify the result.**
  Both $4$ and $8$ are divisible by $4$:
  $$\frac{4 \div 4}{8 \div 4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

**Final Answer**: $\frac{1}{2}$

---

### Example 2 (Medium): Different Denominators (Addition)

**Problem**: Calculate $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}$.

* **Step 1: Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD).**
  The denominators are $3$ and $4$.
  Multiples of $3$: $3, 6, 9, 12, 15, \dots$
  Multiples of $4$: $4, 8, 12, 16, \dots$
  The smallest common multiple is $12$. So, $\text{LCD} = 12$.

* **Step 2: Convert $\frac{1}{3}$ into an equivalent fraction with denominator $12$.**
  To turn $3$ into $12$, multiply by $4$. Multiply both top and bottom by $4$:
  $$\frac{1}{3} = \frac{1 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \frac{4}{12}$$

* **Step 3: Convert $\frac{1}{4}$ into an equivalent fraction with denominator $12$.**
  To turn $4$ into $12$, multiply by $3$. Multiply both top and bottom by $3$:
  $$\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \frac{3}{12}$$

* **Step 4: Add the numerators.**
  $$\frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{4 + 3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$$

* **Step 5: Check if reduction is possible.**
  $7$ is a prime number and does not divide $12$. It cannot be simplified.

**Final Answer**: $\frac{7}{12}$

---

### Example 3 (Medium): Different Denominators (Subtraction)

**Problem**: Calculate $\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4}$.

* **Step 1: Find the LCD.**
  As shown in Example 2, $\text{LCD}(3, 4) = 12$.

* **Step 2: Convert fractions to equivalent fractions.**
  $$\frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{12}, \quad \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{12}$$

* **Step 3: Subtract the numerators.**
  $$\frac{4}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{4 - 3}{12} = \frac{1}{12}$$

**Final Answer**: $\frac{1}{12}$

---

### Example 4 (Challenging): Prime / Coprime Denominators

**Problem**: Calculate $\frac{1}{5} + \frac{2}{11}$.

* **Step 1: Find the LCD.**
  The denominators are $5$ and $11$. Since both are prime numbers, their LCD is simply their product:
  $$5 \times 11 = 55$$

* **Step 2: Convert fractions to equivalent fractions with denominator $55$.**
  * For $\frac{1}{5}$: Multiply top and bottom by $11$:
    $$\frac{1}{5} = \frac{1 \times 11}{5 \times 11} = \frac{11}{55}$$
  * For $\frac{2}{11}$: Multiply top and bottom by $5$:
    $$\frac{2}{11} = \frac{2 \times 5}{11 \times 5} = \frac{10}{55}$$

* **Step 3: Add the numerators.**
  $$\frac{11}{55} + \frac{10}{55} = \frac{11 + 10}{55} = \frac{21}{55}$$

* **Step 4: Check for simplification.**
  The factors of $21$ are $1, 3, 7, 21$. None of these divide $55$.

**Final Answer**: $\frac{21}{55}$

---

### Example 5 (Challenging): Negative Result

**Problem**: Calculate $\frac{2}{7} - \frac{3}{5}$.

* **Step 1: Find the LCD.**
  $\text{LCM}(7, 5) = 7 \times 5 = 35$.

* **Step 2: Convert fractions to equivalent fractions with denominator $35$.**
  $$\frac{2}{7} = \frac{2 \times 5}{7 \times 5} = \frac{10}{35}$$
  $$\frac{3}{5} = \frac{3 \times 7}{5 \times 7} = \frac{21}{35}$$

* **Step 3: Subtract the numerators.**
  $$\frac{10}{35} - \frac{21}{35} = \frac{10 - 21}{35} = \frac{-11}{35} = -\frac{11}{35}$$

**Final Answer**: $-\frac{11}{35}$

## 8. Common Mistakes

Understanding common errors helps you avoid making them yourself!

### Mistake 1: Adding Denominators Together
* **Incorrect Approach**:
  $$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1 + 1}{3 + 4} = \frac{2}{7} \quad \text{[WRONG!]}$$

* **Why it is wrong**: 
  $\frac{2}{7} \approx 0.286$, while $\frac{1}{3} \approx 0.333$. Adding a positive number ($\frac{1}{4}$) to $\frac{1}{3}$ cannot result in a number smaller than $\frac{1}{3}$! Denominators describe piece size; you cannot add piece sizes together.

* **How to fix it**: Keep denominators the same after finding a common denominator.

---

### Mistake 2: Changing the Denominator Without Changing the Numerator
* **Incorrect Approach**:
  $$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} \implies \frac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{2}{12} \quad \text{[WRONG!]}$$

* **Why it is wrong**: 
  Changing $\frac{1}{3}$ into $\frac{1}{12}$ makes the value 4 times smaller. You must multiply **both** the top and bottom by $4$ so that the fraction's value stays identical ($\frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{12}$).

* **How to fix it**: Multiply both numerator and denominator by the same multiplier: $\frac{a \times m}{b \times m}$.

---

### Mistake 3: Forgetting to Simplify the Final Fraction
* **Incorrect Approach**: Leaving $\frac{4}{8}$ as your final answer.
* **Why it is incomplete**: In mathematics, answers should always be expressed in their simplest form.
* **How to fix it**: Divide numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor: $\frac{4 \div 4}{8 \div 4} = \frac{1}{2}$.

---

### Mistake 4: Losing the Negative Sign in Subtraction
* **Incorrect Approach**: 
  $$\frac{10}{35} - \frac{21}{35} = \frac{11}{35} \quad \text{[WRONG!]}$$

* **Why it is wrong**: $10 - 21 = -11$, not $+11$. Since $\frac{21}{35}$ is larger than $\frac{10}{35}$, subtracting it yields a negative value.

* **How to fix it**: Pay close attention to negative signs when subtracting larger numerators from smaller ones.

## 10. Solutions

Here are detailed, step-by-step solutions for each practice problem.

### Solution to Problem 1: $\frac{2}{9} + \frac{5}{9}$
* **Step 1**: The denominators are identical ($9$).
* **Step 2**: Add numerators: $2 + 5 = 7$.
* **Step 3**: Combine over common denominator: $\frac{7}{9}$.
* **Step 4**: $7$ and $9$ share no common factors.
* **Final Answer**: $\frac{7}{9}$

---

### Solution to Problem 2: $\frac{7}{10} - \frac{3}{10}$
* **Step 1**: The denominators are identical ($10$).
* **Step 2**: Subtract numerators: $7 - 3 = 4$.
* **Step 3**: Combine: $\frac{4}{10}$.
* **Step 4**: Simplify by dividing numerator and denominator by $2$:
  $$\frac{4 \div 2}{10 \div 2} = \frac{2}{5}$$
* **Final Answer**: $\frac{2}{5}$

---

### Solution to Problem 3: $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{3}{8}$
* **Step 1**: Find $\text{LCD}(6, 8)$.
  * Multiples of $6$: $6, 12, 18, 24, 30, \dots$
  * Multiples of $8$: $8, 16, 24, 32, \dots$
  * $\text{LCD} = 24$.
* **Step 2**: Convert fractions:
  $$\frac{1}{6} = \frac{1 \times 4}{6 \times 4} = \frac{4}{24}$$
  $$\frac{3}{8} = \frac{3 \times 3}{8 \times 3} = \frac{9}{24}$$
* **Step 3**: Add numerators:
  $$\frac{4}{24} + \frac{9}{24} = \frac{4 + 9}{24} = \frac{13}{24}$$
* **Step 4**: $13$ is prime and does not divide $24$.
* **Final Answer**: $\frac{13}{24}$

---

### Solution to Problem 4: $\frac{4}{5} - \frac{1}{2}$
* **Step 1**: Find $\text{LCD}(5, 2)$. Since $5$ and $2$ are prime, $\text{LCD} = 5 \times 2 = 10$.
* **Step 2**: Convert fractions:
  $$\frac{4}{5} = \frac{4 \times 2}{5 \times 2} = \frac{8}{10}$$
  $$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 5}{2 \times 5} = \frac{5}{10}$$
* **Step 3**: Subtract numerators:
  $$\frac{8}{10} - \frac{5}{10} = \frac{8 - 5}{10} = \frac{3}{10}$$
* **Step 4**: Cannot be simplified further.
* **Final Answer**: $\frac{3}{10}$

---

### Solution to Problem 5: $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{7} - \frac{1}{2}$
* **Step 1**: Find $\text{LCD}(4, 7, 2)$.
  * Multiples of $4$: $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, \dots$
  * Multiples of $7$: $7, 14, 21, 28, \dots$
  * Multiples of $2$: $2, 4, \dots, 28, \dots$
  * $\text{LCD} = 28$.
* **Step 2**: Convert all three fractions:
  $$\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \times 7}{4 \times 7} = \frac{21}{28}$$
  $$\frac{2}{7} = \frac{2 \times 4}{7 \times 4} = \frac{8}{28}$$
  $$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 14}{2 \times 14} = \frac{14}{28}$$
* **Step 3**: Perform operations left to right:
  $$\frac{21 + 8 - 14}{28} = \frac{29 - 14}{28} = \frac{15}{28}$$
* **Step 4**: Factors of $15$ ($3, 5$) do not divide $28$.
* **Final Answer**: $\frac{15}{28}$

---

### Solution to Problem 6: $\frac{1}{6} - \frac{4}{9}$
* **Step 1**: Find $\text{LCD}(6, 9)$.
  * Multiples of $6$: $6, 12, 18, 24, \dots$
  * Multiples of $9$: $9, 18, 27, \dots$
  * $\text{LCD} = 18$.
* **Step 2**: Convert fractions:
  $$\frac{1}{6} = \frac{1 \times 3}{6 \times 3} = \frac{3}{18}$$
  $$\frac{4}{9} = \frac{4 \times 2}{9 \times 2} = \frac{8}{18}$$
* **Step 3**: Subtract numerators:
  $$\frac{3}{18} - \frac{8}{18} = \frac{3 - 8}{18} = \frac{-5}{18} = -\frac{5}{18}$$
* **Step 4**: Cannot be simplified further.
* **Final Answer**: $-\frac{5}{18}$

## 11. Summary

To add or subtract fractions smoothly:

1. **Check the Denominators**: If they are the same, jump straight to adding or subtracting the numerators.
2. **Find a Common Denominator**: If they are different, find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the bottom numbers.
3. **Rewrite as Equivalent Fractions**: Multiply top and bottom of each fraction by whatever number turns its denominator into the common denominator.
4. **Combine Numerators Only**: Never add or subtract the denominators!
5. **Simplify Your Answer**: Divide the top and bottom by their greatest common factor to put the fraction in lowest terms.

---

## 12. Key Things to Remember

* $\frac{a}{c} \pm \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a \pm b}{c}$ (The denominator $c$ never changes during addition/subtraction).
* **Golden Rule of Equivalent Fractions**: Whatever you multiply to the bottom, you MUST multiply to the top!
* **$\text{LCD}(a, b)$** is the smallest number that both $a$ and $b$ divide into evenly.
* **Always check for reduction**: A fraction like $\frac{4}{8}$ should always be simplified to $\frac{1}{2}$.
* **Watch out for negative signs**: Subtracting a larger numerator from a smaller one gives a negative fraction.
