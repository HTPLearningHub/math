# Measures of Central Tendency and Range

## 1. Introduction

When we have a set of numbers, we often want to describe it with a few simple values.

The word **average** usually means a value that represents or describes a larger data set.

There are several ways to describe a data set:

- **Range** tells us how wide the data set is.
- **Mean** is the usual mathematical average.
- **Median** is the middle value.
- **Mode** is the value that occurs most often.

These measures are useful because one data set can contain many numbers, and these values help us summarize the data.

---

## 2. Prerequisite Knowledge

Before learning these ideas, you should understand:

- how to add numbers
- how to subtract numbers
- how to divide numbers
- how to order numbers from smallest to largest
- how to count how many values are in a data set

### Data set

A **data set** is a collection of values.

For example:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 5,\ 7
$$

is a data set containing four values.

---

## 3. Range

### Definition

The **range** tells us how wide a data set is.

To find the range:

$$
\boxed{\text{Range}=\text{Maximum}-\text{Minimum}}
$$

In other words:

1. Find the largest value.
2. Find the smallest value.
3. Subtract the smallest value from the largest value.

### Example

Consider:

$$
0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 5,\ 7
$$

The largest value is:

$$
7
$$

The smallest value is:

$$
0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{Range}=7-0=7
$$

So the range is:

$$
\boxed{7}
$$

### What does the range tell us?

The range tells us the distance between the smallest and largest values.

A larger range generally means that the data values span a wider interval.

### Common mistake

Do not add the minimum and maximum.

Incorrect:

$$
7+0=7
$$

Although this example happens to give the same number, addition is not the rule.

The correct operation is subtraction:

$$
7-0=7
$$

---

## 4. Mean

### Definition

The **mean** is the usual mathematical average.

To find the mean, add all the values and divide by the number of values.

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=\frac{\text{Sum of all values}}{\text{Number of values}}}
$$

If the values are

$$
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n,
$$

then the mean is:

$$
\boxed{\bar{x}=\frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n}}
$$

The symbol $\bar{x}$ is commonly used for the mean.

### Simple example

Find the mean of:

$$
1,\ 2
$$

First add the values:

$$
1+2=3
$$

There are two values, so divide by $2$:

$$
\frac{3}{2}=1.5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=1.5}
$$

The result makes sense because $1.5$ is exactly halfway between $1$ and $2$.

---

## 5. Baseball Example

Suppose you play ten baseball games and record your number of hits in each game.

The values, from least to greatest, are:

$$
0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 5,\ 7
$$

There are:

$$
10
$$

values.

### Step 1: Add all values

$$
0+1+1+2+2+2+3+5+5+7=28
$$

### Step 2: Divide by the number of values

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{28}{10}=2.8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=2.8}
$$

The mean tells us that you had an average of $2.8$ hits per game.

Notice that $2.8$ does not have to be a value that actually appears in the data set. A mean can be a number that is not one of the original observations.

---

## 6. Median

### Definition

The **median** is the middle value of an ordered data set.

The values must first be arranged in ascending order:

$$
\text{smallest}\rightarrow\text{largest}
$$

The method depends on whether there are an odd or even number of values.

---

## 7. Median with an Odd Number of Values

If there are an odd number of values, there is exactly one middle value.

### Example: Three values

Consider:

$$
1,\ 4,\ 8
$$

There are three values.

The middle value is:

$$
4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=4}
$$

### Example: Five values

Consider:

$$
2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 10
$$

There are five values.

The third value is in the middle:

$$
2,\ 3,\ \boxed{5},\ 7,\ 10
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=5}
$$

For any data set with an odd number of values, there is one middle value.

---

## 8. Median with an Even Number of Values

If there is an even number of values, there are two middle values.

In this situation, take the mean of the two middle values.

### Baseball example

Our data set is:

$$
0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 5,\ 7
$$

There are ten values.

The two middle positions are the fifth and sixth positions:

$$
0,\ 1,\ 1,\ \boxed{2,\ 2},\ 3,\ 5,\ 5,\ 7
$$

Both middle values are $2$.

Take their mean:

$$
\frac{2+2}{2}=2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=2}
$$

### What if the two middle values are different?

Suppose the two middle values were $2$ and $3$.

Then:

$$
\text{Median}=\frac{2+3}{2}
$$

$$
=\frac{5}{2}
$$

$$
=2.5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=2.5}
$$

### Important rule

For an even number of values:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=
\frac{\text{two middle values added together}}{2}}
$$

---

## 9. Why Must the Data Be Ordered?

You cannot reliably find the median until the data is arranged in order.

For example, consider:

$$
7,\ 2,\ 10,\ 3,\ 5
$$

The third value is $10$, but $10$ is not the median.

First order the values:

$$
2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 10
$$

Now the middle value is $5$.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=5}
$$

### Common mistake

Do not simply take the value in the middle of the original list.

Always order the data first.

---

## 10. Mode

### Definition

The **mode** is the value that occurs most frequently.

In simple terms, it is the value that appears the most times.

### Baseball example

Consider:

$$
0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 5,\ 7
$$

Count the occurrences:

| Value | Frequency |
|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $1$ |
| $1$ | $2$ |
| $2$ | $3$ |
| $3$ | $1$ |
| $5$ | $2$ |
| $7$ | $1$ |

The value $2$ occurs three times, which is more than any other value.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=2}
$$

---

## 11. The Four Measures Together

For the baseball data:

$$
0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 5,\ 7
$$

we have:

### Range

$$
7-0=7
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Range}=7}
$$

### Mean

$$
\frac{28}{10}=2.8
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=2.8}
$$

### Median

The two middle values are $2$ and $2$:

$$
\frac{2+2}{2}=2
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=2}
$$

### Mode

The most frequent value is $2$:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=2}
$$

So the complete summary is:

| Measure | Value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Range | $7$ | Width from minimum to maximum |
| Mean | $2.8$ | Usual mathematical average |
| Median | $2$ | Middle value |
| Mode | $2$ | Most frequent value |

---

## 12. Why Are There Different Types of Averages?

A single data set can be described in different ways.

The **mean** uses every value in the calculation. This makes it useful when we want the mathematical average of all observations.

However, the mean can be strongly affected by an unusual or **anomalous** value.

An anomalous value is a value that is very different from most of the other values.

The median and mode are often less affected by such extreme values.

---

## 13. Example: Fireworks

Imagine recording the number of fireworks that occur each day in a town during July.

On most days, the number might be:

$$
0
$$

or close to $0$.

But on July 4, there might be hundreds or thousands of fireworks.

For example, imagine a simplified data set:

$$
0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 500
$$

The value $500$ is much larger than the other values.

### Mean

Add the values:

$$
0+0+0+0+1+0+0+0+500=501
$$

There are nine values:

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{501}{9}\approx55.67
$$

So the mean is approximately:

$$
55.67
$$

But does $55.67$ describe a typical day?

Not very well.

Most days have zero fireworks.

### Median

Order the values:

$$
0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 500
$$

The middle value is:

$$
0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=0}
$$

The median gives a much better description of a typical day in this example.

### Mode

The value $0$ appears seven times.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=0}
$$

The mode also describes the typical situation well.

---

## 14. The Effect of Extreme Values

This example shows an important idea.

The mean is sensitive to extreme values.

If one value becomes very large, it can pull the mean upward.

The median is usually more resistant to extreme values because it depends mainly on the position of values after ordering.

The mode depends on frequency, so an extreme value that occurs only once may have little effect on it.

This is why different measures can be useful for different purposes.

---

## 15. When Should You Use Each Measure?

### Mean

Use the mean when you want the mathematical average and the data does not contain extreme values that would make the mean misleading.

Example:

$$
10,\ 11,\ 12,\ 13,\ 14
$$

The values are relatively close together, so the mean gives a useful summary.

### Median

The median can be especially useful when the data contains extreme values.

For example, income data can contain a small number of extremely high incomes. In such situations, the median can better represent a typical person than the mean.

### Mode

The mode is useful when you want to know which value occurs most often.

It can also be useful for categorical data where calculating a numerical mean does not make sense.

For example, if the favorite colors of a group are:

$$
\text{blue, red, blue, green, blue, red}
$$

the mode is **blue** because it occurs most often.

---

## 16. Important Differences

| Measure | What does it tell us? | Main idea |
|---|---|---|
| Range | How wide the data is | Maximum minus minimum |
| Mean | Mathematical average | Add and divide |
| Median | Middle of the ordered data | Find the middle |
| Mode | Most frequent value | Count occurrences |

---

## 17. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Forgetting to divide when finding the mean

If:

$$
2+4+6=12
$$

the mean is not $12$.

There are three values, so:

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{12}{3}=4
$$

---

### Mistake 2: Using the wrong number of values

When finding the mean, divide by the **number of values**, not by the largest value.

For:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8
$$

there are four values, so:

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{2+4+6+8}{4}
$$

---

### Mistake 3: Finding the median before ordering the data

Always put the data in ascending order first.

Incorrect approach:

$$
8,\ 2,\ 5,\ 1,\ 4
$$

Correct approach:

$$
1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 5,\ 8
$$

The median is:

$$
4
$$

---

### Mistake 4: Choosing the largest number as the mode

The mode is not necessarily the largest value.

It is the value that occurs most often.

For:

$$
1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 10
$$

the mode is:

$$
2
$$

not $10$.

---

### Mistake 5: Assuming the mean must be one of the data values

Consider:

$$
1,\ 2
$$

The mean is:

$$
1.5
$$

But $1.5$ is not in the original data set.

That is completely normal.

# 19. Step-by-Step Solutions

## Solution 1

Data:

$$
2,\ 3,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5
$$

### Range

Maximum:

$$
5
$$

Minimum:

$$
2
$$

Therefore:

$$
5-2=3
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Range}=3}
$$

### Mean

Add the values:

$$
2+3+3+4+5=17
$$

There are five values:

$$
\frac{17}{5}=3.4
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=3.4}
$$

### Median

There are five values, so the third value is the middle:

$$
2,\ 3,\ \boxed{3},\ 4,\ 5
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=3}
$$

### Mode

The value $3$ appears twice. Every other value appears once.

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=3}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=8}
$$

Data:

### Range

$$
6-1=5

\boxed{\text{Range}=5}

## Solution 6

Data:

$$
72,\ 75,\ 76,\ 78,\ 79,\ 80,\ 81,\ 95
$$

### Range

$$
95-72=23
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Range}=23}
$$

### Mean

Add all values:

$$
72+75+76+78+79+80+81+95=636
$$

There are eight values:

$$
\frac{636}{8}=79.5
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=79.5}
$$

### Median

There are eight values.

The middle values are the fourth and fifth:

$$
78,\ 79
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{78+79}{2}=\frac{157}{2}=78.5
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=78.5}
$$

### Mode

Every value occurs exactly once.

Therefore, there is **no mode**.

### Which measure is more representative?

The $95$ is relatively high compared with the other scores.

The mean is affected by this high value. The median is less affected.

Therefore, if $95$ is an unusual score, the median may give a better description of the student's typical performance.

## Solution 7

Data:

$$
0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 0,\ 2,\ 0,\ 100
$$

### Mean

Add the values:

$$
0+0+1+0+2+0+100=103
$$

There are seven values:

$$
\frac{103}{7}\approx14.71
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}\approx14.71}
$$

### Median

The data is already ordered.

There are seven values, so the fourth value is the median:

$$
0,\ 0,\ 1,\ \boxed{0},\ 2,\ 0,\ 100
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{\text{Median}=0}
$$

### Mode

The value $0$ occurs four times.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=0}
$$

### Effect of $100$

The value $100$ is much larger than the other values.

It pulls the mean upward:

$$
\text{Mean}\approx14.71
$$

But the median and mode remain $0$.

This demonstrates why the mean can sometimes be a poor description of a typical observation when extreme values are present.

## 20. Summary

There are several useful ways to summarize a data set.

### Range

The range measures the width of the data:

$$
\boxed{\text{Range}=\text{Maximum}-\text{Minimum}}
$$

### Mean

The mean is the usual mathematical average:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=\frac{\text{Sum of values}}{\text{Number of values}}}
$$

### Median

The median is the middle value after the data is ordered.

- With an odd number of values, take the single middle value.
- With an even number of values, take the mean of the two middle values.

### Mode

The mode is the value that occurs most frequently.

### Extreme values

Extreme values can strongly affect the mean.

The median and mode are often less affected by unusual values.

Therefore, the best measure depends on what you are trying to describe.

---

## 21. Key Things to Remember

1. **Range** tells you how wide the data is.

$$
\boxed{\text{Range}=\text{Maximum}-\text{Minimum}}
$$

2. **Mean** is found by adding all values and dividing by the number of values.

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=\frac{\text{Sum}}{\text{Number of values}}}
$$

3. **Median** is the middle value of an ordered data set.

4. For an even number of values, average the two middle values.

5. **Mode** is the most frequently occurring value.

6. Always order the data before finding the median.

7. The mean can be strongly affected by extreme values.

8. The median can be a better measure of a typical value when extreme values are present.

9. Different measures describe different aspects of the same data.

10. There is no single measure that is always the best. Choose the measure that best represents the data and the question you are asking.
