# Algebraic Equations With Variables on Both Sides: A Complete Beginner's Guide

---

## 1. Introduction

In basic mathematics, you often start by solving simple equations where an unknown number—called a **variable** (usually written as $x$)—appears on only one side of the equals sign. For example, in $x + 5 = 9$, it is easy to see that $x = 4$.

However, as mathematical problems grow more realistic, the variable $x$ will frequently appear on **both sides** of the equals sign, such as:

$$4x + 3 = x - 6$$

Do not be intimidated by this! The fundamental goal of algebra remains exactly the same: **isolate the variable** so that $x$ ends up alone on one side of the equation, equal to a specific number on the other side ($x = \text{number}$).

This guide breaks down the process step-by-step using simple language, clear rules, practical examples, visual guides, common pitfalls to avoid, and practice problems with full solutions.

---

## 2. Prerequisite Knowledge

Before tackling equations with variables on both sides, let us review five essential building blocks of algebra.

### A. What is a Variable and a Constant?
* **Variable**: A letter (such as $x$, $y$, or $n$) that acts as a placeholder for an unknown numerical value.
* **Coefficient**: The number attached directly in front of a variable by multiplication. In $4x$, the number $4$ is the coefficient of $x$. (Note: $x$ by itself means $1x$).
* **Constant**: A fixed number without any variable attached (e.g., $+3$, $-6$, $7$).

### B. The Balance Scale Concept
An equation is like a **balanced scale**. The equals sign ($=$) states that the left side and the right side have equal value. 

> **The Golden Rule of Algebra:** Whatever mathematical operation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division) you perform on one side of an equation, you **must** perform on the exact same way to the entire other side to keep the equation balanced.

```
       [ Left Side ]  === (  =  ) ===  [ Right Side ]
             |                               |
    Do + / - / * / ÷               Do identical + / - / * / ÷
```

### C. Inverse (Opposite) Operations
To move numbers or terms across the equals sign, we use **inverse operations** to "undo" them:
* **Addition ($+$)** is undone by **Subtraction ($-$)**.
* **Subtraction ($-$)** is undone by **Addition ($+$)**.
* **Multiplication ($\cdot$)** is undone by **Division ($\div$)**.
* **Division ($\div$)** is undone by **Multiplication ($\cdot$)**.

### D. Combining Like Terms
**Like terms** are terms that contain the exact same variable raised to the same power. 
* $4x$ and $1x$ are like terms. We can add or subtract their coefficients: $4x - 1x = 3x$.
* Think of it like real objects: $4\text{ apples} - 1\text{ apple} = 3\text{ apples}$. The variable $x$ does not disappear when subtracting coefficients!
* $3x$ and $7$ are **not** like terms (one has $x$, the other is just a number). You cannot add them into $10x$.

### E. The Distributive Property
When a number is written outside parentheses, such as $3(2x + 1)$, it multiplies **every term** inside the parentheses:

$$a(b + c) = a \cdot b + a \cdot c$$

$$\text{Example: } 3(2x + 1) = (3 \cdot 2x) + (3 \cdot 1) = 6x + 3$$

## 4. Main Concepts & Strategy

When solving algebraic equations, having a systematic strategy prevents confusion and avoids calculation errors.

### General 5-Step Strategy for Solving Linear Equations

1. **Clear Parentheses (Distribute):** Use the distributive property to eliminate any parentheses.
2. **Clear Fractions (If Any):** Multiply all terms on both sides by the denominator to eliminate fractions.
3. **Gather Variable Terms on One Side:** Move all terms containing $x$ to one side (usually the left) by adding or subtracting variable terms.
4. **Gather Constant Terms on the Other Side:** Move all plain numbers to the opposite side by adding or subtracting constants.
5. **Isolate the Variable (Divide or Multiply):** Divide both sides by the coefficient of $x$ to get $x$ completely by itself.

---

### Why Do Addition/Subtraction Before Multiplication/Division?

When isolating a variable in a multi-step equation, it is usually much cleaner to perform **addition and subtraction before multiplication and division**.

#### Comparison Demonstration:
Consider the equation:

$$3x + 7 = 16$$

* **Correct & Easy Approach (Subtract $7$ first):**
  $$3x + 7 - 7 = 16 - 7$$
  $$3x = 9$$
  $$\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{9}{3} \implies x = 3$$

* **Why Dividing First Makes Things Harder:**
  If you divide by $3$ first, you must divide the **entire left side**, not just $3x$:
  $$\frac{3x + 7}{3} = \frac{16}{3} \implies x + \frac{7}{3} = \frac{16}{3}$$
  This introduces fractions unnecessarily, making the problem harder than it needs to be!

---

### The Shortcut: "Pulling Across the Equals Sign"

When dealing with a fraction where one entire side is divided by a number, such as:

$$\frac{3 - 3x}{4} = 6x + 3$$

Instead of writing out "multiply both sides by $4$" and writing extra steps, you can conceptualize pulling the denominator $4$ up to the numerator of the opposite side as a multiplier:

$$3 - 3x = 4 \cdot (6x + 3)$$

> **Rule:** Any quantity in the denominator of one side can be pulled up to multiply the entire expression on the other side. Make sure to put parentheses around the target expression!

## 5. Formulas and Rules

### 1. Combining Like Terms Formula
$$ax + bx = (a + b)x$$
$$ax - bx = (a - b)x$$

### 2. Distributive Law
$$a(b + c) = ab + ac$$
$$a(b - c) = ab - ac$$

### 3. Fraction Clearing Rule
$$\frac{A}{B} = C \implies A = B \cdot C$$

### 4. Equation Symmetry Property
If $A = B$, then $B = A$. 
*(If you end up with $-9 = 27x$, you can flip it to $27x = -9$ because equations can be read in either direction).*

## 6. Step-by-Step Examples

Here are three detailed walkthroughs ranging from basic multi-step equations to complex equations with variables on both sides, fractions, and parentheses.

---

### Example 1: Basic Two-Step Equation
**Problem:** Solve for $x$:

$$3x + 7 = 16$$

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

* **Step 1: Undo the addition of $7$.**
  Subtract $7$ from both sides to keep the variable term $3x$ isolated on the left:
  $$3x + 7 - 7 = 16 - 7$$
  $$3x = 9$$

* **Step 2: Undo the multiplication by $3$.**
  Divide both sides by $3$:
  $$\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{9}{3}$$
  $$x = 3$$

* **Step 3: Check your answer.**
  Substitute $x = 3$ back into the original equation:
  $$3(3) + 7 = 9 + 7 = 16 \quad \checkmark \text{ (Correct!)}$$

---

### Example 2: Variables on Both Sides
**Problem:** Solve for $x$:

$$4x + 3 = x - 6$$

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

* **Step 1: Gather all $x$ terms on the left side.**
  We have $4x$ on the left and $1x$ on the right. Subtract $x$ from both sides:
  $$(4x - x) + 3 = (x - x) - 6$$
  $$3x + 3 = -6$$
  *(Note: $4x - x = 3x$. Just like $4\text{ apples} - 1\text{ apple} = 3\text{ apples}$, the $x$ remains).*

* **Step 2: Move the constant number ($+3$) to the right side.**
  Subtract $3$ from both sides:
  $$3x + 3 - 3 = -6 - 3$$
  $$3x = -9$$
  *(Note: $-6 - 3 = -9$. When subtracting from a negative number, you move further into the negatives).*

* **Step 3: Isolate $x$ by dividing by $3$.**
  $$\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{-9}{3}$$
  $$x = -3$$

* **Step 4: Check your answer.**
  Substitute $x = -3$ into both sides of the original equation:
  * Left side: $4(-3) + 3 = -12 + 3 = -9$
  * Right side: $(-3) - 6 = -9$
  Both sides equal $-9$, so $x = -3$ is correct! $\checkmark$

---

### Example 3: Challenging Equation with Parentheses and Fractions
**Problem:** Solve for $x$:

$$\frac{3 - 3x}{4} = 3(2x + 1)$$

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

* **Step 1: Distribute to expand the parentheses on the right side.**
  $$3 \cdot (2x + 1) = 6x + 3$$
  The equation now looks like:
  $$\frac{3 - 3x}{4} = 6x + 3$$

* **Step 2: Clear the fraction by multiplying both sides by $4$.**
  Pull the denominator $4$ up to multiply the entire right side:
  $$3 - 3x = 4(6x + 3)$$
  Distribute $4$ to both terms inside the parentheses:
  $$3 - 3x = 24x + 12$$

* **Step 3: Gather variable ($x$) terms on one side.**
  Add $3x$ to both sides to move all variable terms to the right:
  $$3 - 3x + 3x = 24x + 3x + 12$$
  $$3 = 27x + 12$$

* **Step 4: Gather constant numbers on the opposite side.**
  Subtract $12$ from both sides:
  $$3 - 12 = 27x + 12 - 12$$
  $$-9 = 27x$$

* **Step 5: Flip the equation for standard readability.**
  $$27x = -9$$

* **Step 6: Isolate $x$ by dividing by $27$ and simplify.**
  $$x = \frac{-9}{27}$$
  Divide numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor ($9$):
  $$x = -\frac{1}{3}$$

* **Step 7: Check your answer.**
  Substitute $x = -\frac{1}{3}$ into the original equation:
  * Left side: $\frac{3 - 3(-1/3)}{4} = \frac{3 + 1}{4} = \frac{4}{4} = 1$
  * Right side: $3(2(-1/3) + 1) = 3(-2/3 + 3/3) = 3(1/3) = 1$
  Both sides equal $1$, so $x = -\frac{1}{3}$ is correct! $\checkmark$

## 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Dividing only one term instead of the whole expression
* ❌ **Wrong:** In $3x + 7 = 16$, dividing by $3$ to get $x + 7 = 5.33$.
* ✅ **Right:** Subtract $7$ first ($3x = 9$), then divide by $3$ ($x = 3$). If you divide first, you must divide **every** term by $3$: $\frac{3x}{3} + \frac{7}{3} = \frac{16}{3}$.

### Mistake 2: Dropping the variable when combining like terms
* ❌ **Wrong:** $4x - x = 3$ (thinking $x - x = 0$ leaves only $3$).
* ✅ **Right:** $4x - 1x = 3x$. Remember: $4\text{ items} - 1\text{ item} = 3\text{ items}$. The variable $x$ stays!

### Mistake 3: Forgetting to distribute to ALL terms inside parentheses
* ❌ **Wrong:** $3(2x + 1) = 6x + 1$ (forgetting to multiply $3 \times 1$).
* ✅ **Right:** $3(2x + 1) = 3(2x) + 3(1) = 6x + 3$.

### Mistake 4: Arithmetic sign errors with negative numbers
* ❌ **Wrong:** In $3x + 3 = -6$, subtracting $3$ to get $3x = -3$ (incorrectly calculating $-6 - 3$).
* ✅ **Right:** $-6 - 3 = -9$. Subtracting a positive number from a negative number moves further left on the number line.

## 10. Solutions

### Solution to Problem 1
$$2x + 5 = 11$$
1. Subtract $5$ from both sides:
   $$2x = 11 - 5 \implies 2x = 6$$
2. Divide by $2$:
   $$x = \frac{6}{2} \implies x = 3$$

---

### Solution to Problem 2
$$5x - 4 = 2x + 5$$
1. Subtract $2x$ from both sides:
   $$5x - 2x - 4 = 5 \implies 3x - 4 = 5$$
2. Add $4$ to both sides:
   $$3x = 5 + 4 \implies 3x = 9$$
3. Divide by $3$:
   $$x = \frac{9}{3} \implies x = 3$$

---

### Solution to Problem 3
$$3(x - 2) = 2x + 4$$
1. Distribute $3$ on the left side:
   $$3x - 6 = 2x + 4$$
2. Subtract $2x$ from both sides:
   $$3x - 2x - 6 = 4 \implies x - 6 = 4$$
3. Add $6$ to both sides:
   $$x = 4 + 6 \implies x = 10$$

---

### Solution to Problem 4
$$7x + 2 = 3x - 14$$
1. Subtract $3x$ from both sides:
   $$7x - 3x + 2 = -14 \implies 4x + 2 = -14$$
2. Subtract $2$ from both sides:
   $$4x = -14 - 2 \implies 4x = -16$$
3. Divide by $4$:
   $$x = \frac{-16}{4} \implies x = -4$$

---

### Solution to Problem 5
$$\frac{5 - 2x}{3} = 2(x + 3)$$
1. Distribute $2$ on the right side:
   $$\frac{5 - 2x}{3} = 2x + 6$$
2. Multiply both sides by $3$ to clear the denominator:
   $$5 - 2x = 3(2x + 6) \implies 5 - 2x = 6x + 18$$
3. Add $2x$ to both sides:
   $$5 = 6x + 2x + 18 \implies 5 = 8x + 18$$
4. Subtract $18$ from both sides:
   $$5 - 18 = 8x \implies -13 = 8x$$
5. Flip equation and divide by $8$:
   $$8x = -13 \implies x = -\frac{13}{8}$$

---

### Solution to Problem 6
$$4(2x - 1) - 3x = 2(x + 6)$$
1. Distribute on both sides:
   $$(8x - 4) - 3x = 2x + 12$$
2. Combine like terms on the left side ($8x - 3x$):
   $$5x - 4 = 2x + 12$$
3. Subtract $2x$ from both sides:
   $$5x - 2x - 4 = 12 \implies 3x - 4 = 12$$
4. Add $4$ to both sides:
   $$3x = 12 + 4 \implies 3x = 16$$
5. Divide by $3$:
   $$x = \frac{16}{3}$$

## 11. Summary

Solving algebraic equations with variables on both sides requires a methodical approach:
1. **Remove barriers**: Expand parentheses using the distributive property and clear fraction denominators by multiplying.
2. **Consolidate variables**: Add or subtract variable terms so that all $x$'s gather on one side.
3. **Consolidate constants**: Add or subtract numbers so that all constant values gather on the opposite side.
4. **Isolate $x$**: Divide by the coefficient attached to $x$.
5. **Verify**: Plug your answer back into the original equation to ensure both sides balance.

---

## 12. Key Things to Remember

* ⚖️ **Keep it Balanced:** Whatever operation you do to one side of an equation, do to the other side.
* ➕ **Do $+$ and $-$ before $\times$ and $\div$:** It avoids messier calculations with fractions.
* 🍎 **Variable terms stay variables:** $4x - x = 3x$ (not $3$).
* ✖️ **Distribute completely:** $a(b + c) = ab + ac$. Do not forget the second term!
* 🔁 **Flip freely:** $A = B$ means $B = A$. If you get $-9 = 27x$, rewrite it as $27x = -9$.
* ✔️ **Always Check Your Answer:** Substitute your result back into the original equation to verify accuracy.
