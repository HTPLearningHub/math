# Decimals: Notation and Operations

## 1. Introduction
In our daily lives, we often encounter quantities that are not whole numbers. For example, when sharing food, measuring height, or counting money, we deal with parts of a whole [12]. 

Before learning about decimals, we represent parts of a whole using **fractions** (such as $\frac{1}{10}$) or **mixed numbers** (such as $1 \frac{1}{10}$) [12]. However, writing fractions can sometimes be inconvenient when performing calculations [12]. 

**Decimals** give us a simple, powerful way to write fractional numbers using our standard base-$10$ system [12]. Instead of creating a new way of counting, decimals extend the same place value rules we already use for whole numbers ($1$, $10$, $100$) to represent smaller parts ($0.1$, $0.01$, $0.001$) [12]. Understanding decimals helps us work with money, scientific measurements, and data accurately [12].

---

## 2. Prerequisite Knowledge

To understand decimals easily, let us review three foundational ideas:

### A. Whole Number Place Values
In our number system, the position (place) of a digit determines its value [12].
For example, in the number $325$:
* The digit $5$ is in the **ones (units)** place, so it represents $5 \times 1 = 5$ [12].
* The digit $2$ is in the **tens** place, so it represents $2 \times 10 = 20$ [12].
* The digit $3$ is in the **hundreds** place, so it represents $3 \times 100 = 300$ [12].

### B. The Base-10 Scaling Rule
Our number system is based on the number $10$ [12]. 
* As you move **one position to the left**, the place value becomes **$10$ times greater** ($1 \to 10 \to 100 \to 1000$) [12].
* As you move **one position to the right**, the place value becomes **$10$ times smaller** ($\frac{1}{10}$ of the previous place) [12].

### C. Basic Fractions
A fraction represents parts of a whole object divided into equal pieces [12].
* If a whole pizza is cut into $10$ equal slices, $1$ slice represents $\frac{1}{10}$ (one tenth) of the pizza [12].
* If you have $10$ slices, you have $\frac{10}{10} = 1$ whole pizza [12].

---

## 3. Important Definitions

Here are the key mathematical terms used when working with decimals:

* **Decimal Point ($\text{.}$)**: A small dot placed between the ones place and the tenths place [12]. It separates the whole number part from the fractional part [12].
* **Decimal Place**: The position of a digit located to the right of the decimal point [12].
* **Tenths Place**: The first position to the right of the decimal point [12]. It represents parts divided into $10$ equal pieces ($\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$) [12].
* **Hundredths Place**: The second position to the right of the decimal point [12]. It represents parts divided into $100$ equal pieces ($\frac{1}{100} = 0.01$) [12].
* **Thousandths Place**: The third position to the right of the decimal point [12]. It represents parts divided into $1000$ equal pieces ($\frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$) [12].
* **Magnitude**: The size or scale of a number, determined by its place value [12].

---

## 4. Main Concepts

### Concept 1: Extending Place Values Beyond Ones
When we have a quantity between two whole numbers (such as $1 \frac{1}{10}$ pizzas), we do not need to use fraction notation [12]. We place a decimal point to the right of the ones (units) digit and add new place values to represent the fractional part [12].

For $1 \frac{1}{10}$ pizzas:
* We have $1$ whole pizza in the ones place [12].
* We have $1$ extra slice ($\frac{1}{10}$) in the tenths place [12].
* Written as a decimal, this is $1.1$ (read as *"one point one"* or *"one and one tenth"*) [12].

### Concept 2: The Same Digit Reports Different Magnitudes
The symbol $1$ always looks the same, but its actual value depends on the position it occupies [12]:
* In $100$, the digit $1$ represents $100$ wholes [12].
* In $1$, the digit $1$ represents $1$ whole unit [12].
* In $0.1$, the digit $1$ represents $1$ tenth ($\frac{1}{10}$) [12].
* In $0.01$, the digit $1$ represents $1$ hundredth ($\frac{1}{100}$) [12].
* In $0.001$, the digit $1$ represents $1$ thousandth ($\frac{1}{1000}$) [12].

### Concept 3: Performing Operations (Addition)
When adding decimal numbers, we follow the exact same logic as adding whole numbers [12]. 

**The Golden Rule of Decimal Addition**: You must align digits by their **place values** [12]. To do this automatically, simply align the **decimal points** vertically [12].

For example, to calculate $2.3 + 1.4$:
1. Add the digits in the **tenths place**: $3 \text{ tenths} + 4 \text{ tenths} = 7 \text{ tenths}$ ($0.7$) [12].
2. Add the digits in the **ones place**: $2 \text{ ones} + 1 \text{ one} = 3 \text{ ones}$ ($3$) [12].
3. Combine them to get $3.7$ [12].

This is directly analogous to adding $23 + 14 = 37$, but the decimal point adjusts the magnitude to tenths [12].

## 5. Formulas and Rules

### Rule 1: The Place Value Chart Rule
Moving left multiplies value by $10$; moving right divides value by $10$ [12]:

$$\dots \quad \text{Hundreds} (100) \quad \text{Tens} (10) \quad \text{Ones} (1) \quad \mathbf{.} \quad \text{Tenths} (0.1) \quad \text{Hundredths} (0.01) \quad \text{Thousandths} (0.001) \quad \dots$$

### Rule 2: Equivalent Trailing Zeros
Adding zeros to the very right of a decimal number does not change its value:
$$0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500 = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{50}{100}$$

### Rule 3: Vertical Decimal Addition Steps
1. Write the numbers vertically.
2. Line up the **decimal points** so place values match [12].
3. Fill empty spaces on the right with zeros if helpful.
4. Add the digits column by column from right to left [12].
5. Place the decimal point in the answer directly below the existing decimal points [12].

$$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
  2.3 \\
+\; 1.4 \\
\hline
  3.7
\end{array}$$

## 6. Step-by-Step Examples

### Example 1 (Easy): Writing Decimals from Visual Context
**Problem**: You have $2$ whole pizzas and $3$ extra slices (where $1$ whole pizza has $10$ slices). Write this quantity as a decimal [12].

**Step-by-Step Explanation**:
1. Identify the whole units: You have $2$ whole pizzas. Place $2$ in the **ones** place.
2. Place the decimal point after the ones digit: $2.$
3. Identify the fractional parts: $3$ slices out of $10$ equals $\frac{3}{10}$.
4. Place $3$ in the **tenths** place (first position after the decimal point).
5. **Final Answer**: $2.3$ pizzas [12].

---

### Example 2 (Easy): Adding Decimals Without Regrouping
**Problem**: Calculate $2.3 + 1.4$ [12].

**Step-by-Step Explanation**:
1. Write the numbers vertically and line up the decimal points [12]:
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     2.3 \\
   +\; 1.4 \\
   \hline
   \end{array}$$
2. Add the tenths column ($3 + 4$):
   $$3 \text{ tenths} + 4 \text{ tenths} = 7 \text{ tenths} \quad (0.7)$$
3. Add the ones column ($2 + 1$):
   $$2 \text{ ones} + 1 \text{ one} = 3 \text{ ones} \quad (3)$$
4. Write the decimal point directly in line with the problem [12]:
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     2.3 \\
   +\; 1.4 \\
   \hline
     3.7
   \end{array}$$
5. **Final Answer**: $3.7$ [12].

---

### Example 3 (Medium): Adding Decimals with Different Numbers of Digits
**Problem**: Calculate $5.2 + 3.14$.

**Step-by-Step Explanation**:
1. Line up the numbers by their decimal points:
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     5.2\phantom{0} \\
   +\; 3.14 \\
   \hline
   \end{array}$$
2. Pad $5.2$ with a trailing zero so both numbers have two decimal places ($5.20 = 5.2$):
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     5.20 \\
   +\; 3.14 \\
   \hline
   \end{array}$$
3. Add the hundredths column: $0 + 4 = 4$ hundredths.
4. Add the tenths column: $2 + 1 = 3$ tenths.
5. Add the ones column: $5 + 3 = 8$ ones.
6. Place the decimal point in the result:
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     5.20 \\
   +\; 3.14 \\
   \hline
     8.34
   \end{array}$$
7. **Final Answer**: $8.34$.

---

### Example 4 (Challenging): Decimal Addition with Regrouping (Carrying)
**Problem**: Calculate $4.85 + 2.67$.

**Step-by-Step Explanation**:
1. Line up the decimal points vertically:
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     4.85 \\
   +\; 2.67 \\
   \hline
   \end{array}$$
2. Add hundredths ($5 + 7 = 12$ hundredths):
   * $12 \text{ hundredths} = 1 \text{ tenth} + 2 \text{ hundredths}$.
   * Write $2$ in hundredths column, carry $1$ to tenths column.
3. Add tenths ($1 \text{ carried} + 8 + 6 = 15 \text{ tenths}$):
   * $15 \text{ tenths} = 1 \text{ one} + 5 \text{ tenths}$.
   * Write $5$ in tenths column, carry $1$ to ones column.
4. Add ones ($1 \text{ carried} + 4 + 2 = 7 \text{ ones}$):
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     \small{1}\;\small{1}\;\;\; \\
     4.85 \\
   +\; 2.67 \\
   \hline
     7.52
   \end{array}$$
5. **Final Answer**: $7.52$.

## 7. Visual Explanations

Visual representations make decimal place values intuitive.

### Diagram 1: Place Value System
The place value system extends symmetrically around the ones place and decimal point:

| Hundreds ($100$) | Tens ($10$) | Ones / Units ($1$) | DECIMAL POINT ($\text{.}$) | Tenths ($0.1 = \frac{1}{10}$) | Hundredths ($0.01 = \frac{1}{100}$) | Thousandths ($0.001 = \frac{1}{1000}$) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $\leftarrow 10 \times \text{ larger}$ | $\leftarrow 10 \times \text{ larger}$ | Base unit | $\mathbf{.}$ | $\frac{1}{10} \text{ smaller} \rightarrow$ | $\frac{1}{10} \text{ smaller} \rightarrow$ | $\frac{1}{10} \text{ smaller} \rightarrow$ |

### Diagram 2: Decimal Number Line (Tenths)
Below is a visual number line showing how numbers between $0$ and $2$ are divided into $10$ equal steps (tenths) [12]:

### Diagram 3: Grid Representation of Hundredths
Imagine a large square representing $1$ whole unit, divided into $100$ small equal squares [12]:
* **$1$ small square** $= \frac{1}{100} = 0.01$ (one hundredth) [12].
* **$1$ full row or column ($10$ small squares)** $= \frac{10}{100} = \frac{1}{10} = 0.10 = 0.1$ (one tenth) [12].
* **Entire $100$-square grid** $= \frac{100}{100} = 1.0$ ($1$ whole) [12].

## 8. Common Mistakes

Understanding common errors helps avoid costly calculation mistakes:

### Mistake 1: Aligning Digits to the Right Instead of Decimal Points
* **Incorrect Approach**:
  $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
    2.3 \\
  +\; 1.45 \\
  \hline
    3.75 \quad \text{(WRONG: 3 was added to 4, but 3 is tenths and 4 is hundredths!)}
  \end{array}$$
* **Why it is wrong**: The digit $3$ is in the tenths place ($0.3$), but $4$ in $1.45$ is in the tenths place! Adding $3$ to $5$ mixes different place values.
* **Correct Approach**: Pad $2.3$ as $2.30$ and align decimal points:
  $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
    2.30 \\
  +\; 1.45 \\
  \hline
    3.75 \quad \text{(Correct sum is } 2.30 + 1.45 = 3.75\text{)}
  \end{array}$$

### Mistake 2: Thinking More Decimal Digits Means a Larger Number
* **Incorrect Belief**: Thinking $0.125$ is greater than $0.5$ because $125 > 5$.
* **Why it is wrong**: Compare place values from left to right. $0.5$ has $5$ tenths ($\frac{5}{10}$), whereas $0.125$ has only $1$ tenth ($\frac{1}{10}$). 
* **Correct Comparison**: $0.500 > 0.125$, so $0.5$ is significantly larger than $0.125$.

### Mistake 3: Confusing Tenths and Hundredths
* **Incorrect Writing**: Writing "three hundredths" as $0.3$.
* **Why it is wrong**: $0.3$ is $3$ tenths ($\frac{3}{10}$). 
* **Correct Writing**: Three hundredths ($\frac{3}{100}$) requires a zero in the tenths place: $0.03$.

## 9. Practice Problems

Test your understanding with these practice problems arranged from easy to challenging:

1. **Problem 1 (Easy)**: Convert the following fractions to decimal notation:
   a) $\frac{4}{10}$
   b) $3 \frac{7}{10}$
   c) $\frac{9}{100}$

2. **Problem 2 (Easy)**: Identify the place value of the underlined digit:
   a) $4.\underline{6}2$
   b) $0.0\underline{8}$
   c) $\underline{5}.19$

3. **Problem 3 (Medium)**: Calculate the sum: $4.5 + 3.2$.

4. **Problem 4 (Medium)**: Calculate the sum: $12.4 + 3.18$.

5. **Problem 5 (Challenging)**: Maria bought $1.75$ kg of apples and $2.6$ kg of oranges. What is the total weight of the fruit she bought?

---

## 10. Solutions

### Solution to Problem 1:
a) $\frac{4}{10} = 0.4$ ($4$ in the tenths place).
b) $3 \frac{7}{10} = 3.7$ ($3$ in the ones place, $7$ in the tenths place).
c) $\frac{9}{100} = 0.09$ ($0$ in the tenths place, $9$ in the hundredths place).

### Solution to Problem 2:
a) In $4.\underline{6}2$, $6$ is in the **tenths** place.
b) In $0.0\underline{8}$, $8$ is in the **hundredths** place.
c) In $\underline{5}.19$, $5$ is in the **ones (units)** place.

### Solution to Problem 3:
Line up the decimal points vertically:
$$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
  4.5 \\
+\; 3.2 \\
\hline
  7.7
\end{array}$$
**Answer**: $7.7$.

### Solution to Problem 4:
Pad $12.4$ with a trailing zero ($12.40$) and align decimal points:
$$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
  12.40 \\
+\; 3.18 \\
\hline
  15.58
\end{array}$$
**Answer**: $15.58$.

### Solution to Problem 5:
1. Identify the given numbers: $1.75$ kg and $2.6$ kg.
2. Pad $2.6$ as $2.60$ and align decimal points vertically:
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     1.75 \\
   +\; 2.60 \\
   \hline
   \end{array}$$
3. Add hundredths: $5 + 0 = 5$.
4. Add tenths: $7 + 6 = 13$ tenths ($3$ tenths, carry $1$ to ones).
5. Add ones: $1 \text{ (carried)} + 1 + 2 = 4$ ones.
   $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
     \small{1}\;\;\; \\
     1.75 \\
   +\; 2.60 \\
   \hline
     4.35
   \end{array}$$
**Answer**: The total weight of fruit is $4.35$ kg.

## 11. Summary
* **Decimals** extend our standard base-$10$ place value system to represent quantities smaller than $1$ whole [12].
* A **decimal point** separates the whole number part on the left from the fractional part on the right [12].
* Going right from the decimal point, place values decrease by a factor of $10$: tenths ($0.1$), hundredths ($0.01$), thousandths ($0.001$) [12].
* When **adding decimals**, always align the decimal points vertically so you add digits with identical place values [12].

---

## 12. Key Things to Remember
* $\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$ (tenths)
* $\frac{1}{100} = 0.01$ (hundredths)
* $\frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$ (thousandths)
* Moving one position left $= \times 10$ larger [12].
* Moving one position right $= \div 10$ smaller ($\times 0.1$) [12].
* **Golden Rule for Addition**: Always align decimal points vertically [12]!
* Pad trailing zeros (e.g. $2.3 = 2.30$) to prevent place value misalignment.
