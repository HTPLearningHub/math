# Converting Between Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages
*A Complete, Beginner-Friendly, and Self-Contained Mathematics Manual*

---

### 1. Introduction

In mathematics and everyday life, we often need to express parts of a whole [12]. For example, if you eat half of a pizza, buy a shirt on sale, or calculate sales tax, you are working with fractional parts [12, 13]. 

There are three primary ways to express these values [12]:
1. **Fractions** (e.g., $\frac{3}{10}$) [12]
2. **Decimals** (e.g., $0.3$) [12, 13]
3. **Percentages** (e.g., $30\%$) [12]

The most important fundamental concept to understand is that **fractions, decimals, and percentages are simply three different visual languages used to express the exact same mathematical value** [12, 15]. Being able to convert smoothly between these forms allows you to choose the easiest format for any calculation, comparison, or real-world scenario [12, 15].

---

### 2. Prerequisite Knowledge

Before learning conversion techniques, let us review the basic building blocks.

#### A. Fraction Structure (Numerator and Denominator)
A fraction represents a part of a whole quantity [12]. It is written as:
$$\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}}$$

* **Numerator (Top Number):** Represents how many equal parts you have or are considering [12].
* **Denominator (Bottom Number):** Represents the total number of equal parts that make up one complete whole [12].

*Example:* In the fraction $\frac{3}{10}$, the whole is divided into $10$ equal parts, and you have $3$ of those parts [12].

#### B. Reducing (Simplifying) Fractions
A fraction is in its **simplest form** (or lowest terms) when the numerator and denominator share no common factors other than $1$.
To reduce a fraction, divide both the top and bottom numbers by their Greatest Common Factor (GCF) [12].

* **Hot Tip (Division Shortcut):** When both the numerator and denominator end in zero, you can cross out one zero from each number [12]. Crossing out a trailing zero is mathematically identical to dividing both numbers by $10$ [12].

$$\frac{30}{100} = \frac{30 \div 10}{100 \div 10} = \frac{3}{10}$$

#### C. Place Value in Decimals
Decimals use base-10 place values to represent parts of a whole [12, 13]. The position of a digit to the right of the decimal point indicates its fractional denominator:
* **First place to the right:** Tenths place ($\frac{1}{10}$ or $0.1$) [12, 13]
* **Second place to the right:** Hundredths place ($\frac{1}{100}$ or $0.01$) [12, 13]
* **Third place to the right:** Thousandths place ($\frac{1}{1000}$ or $0.001$) [13]

*Example:* In the number $0.3$, the digit $3$ sits in the tenths place, so $0.3 = \frac{3}{10}$ [12, 13].

#### D. The Meaning of "Percent"
The word **percent** comes from the Latin *per centum*, which literally means "out of one hundred" [12]. 
* $100\%$ represents a complete whole ($100$ out of $100$) [12].
* Any percentage $P\%$ can instantly be written as a fraction with a denominator of $100$: 
$$P\% = \frac{P}{100}$$ [12, 15]

#### E. Orders of Magnitude
An **order of magnitude** refers to a scale of magnitude where each step represents a multiplication or division by $10$ [13].
* The number $100$ is $2$ orders of magnitude greater than $1$ ($10 \times 10 = 100$) [13].
* Multiplying or dividing by $100$ shifts the decimal point by **two positions** [13, 14].

---

### 3. Important Definitions

* **Fraction:** A number representing a ratio of two integers in the form $\frac{a}{b}$, where $b \neq 0$ [12].
* **Decimal:** A number written in base-10 notation, where a decimal point separates the whole number portion from the fractional portion [12, 13].
* **Percentage:** A fraction expressed specifically as a quantity out of $100$, denoted using the percent symbol ($\%$) [12].
* **Equivalent Values:** Numbers expressed in different formats (fractions, decimals, percentages) that represent the exact same quantity [12, 15].
* **Sales Tax:** A percentage fee added to the purchase price of goods or services by a government [13].

---

### 4. Main Concepts

#### The Equivalence Principle
Every rational number can be expressed interchangeably as a fraction, a decimal, or a percentage [12, 15]. 

$$\text{Fraction } \left(\frac{3}{10}\right) \iff \text{Decimal } (0.3) \iff \text{Percentage } (30\%)$$ [12, 13]

#### Why Use Different Forms?
1. **Fractions** are best for exact division, algebraic equations, and recipes where dividing into equal slices is needed [12].
2. **Decimals** are ideal for monetary calculations, digital equipment, and scientific measurements [13, 14].
3. **Percentages** are perfect for comparing proportions across groups of different sizes, displaying growth rates, interest rates, and retail discounts [12, 13].

### 5. Formulas and Rules

Here are the step-by-step rules and formulas for converting between all three forms.

---

#### Rule 1: Percentage $\to$ Fraction

$$\text{Fraction} = \frac{\text{Percent Value}}{100}$$ [12]

* **What it means:** Replace the percentage symbol ($\%$) with a denominator of $100$ [12].
* **Why it works:** Percentages are defined as parts out of $100$ [12].
* **When to use it:** When you need to express a percentage as a simplified ratio or fraction [12].
* **How to use it:**
  1. Write the percentage number as the numerator over $100$ [12].
  2. Drop the $\% \text{ symbol}$ [12].
  3. Simplify the fraction by dividing by common factors (or use the zero-canceling shortcut) [12].
* **Common Mistake:** Forgetting to simplify the final fraction [12].

---

#### Rule 2: Percentage $\to$ Decimal

$$\text{Decimal} = \text{Percent Value} \div 100$$ [13]

* **Hot Tip (Shortcut):** Shift the decimal point **two places to the left** [13].
* **What it means:** Convert a percentage out of $100$ into base-10 place value [13].
* **Why it works:** Dividing by $100$ reduces a number by $2$ orders of magnitude [13].
* **When to use it:** When calculating tax, interest, or discounts on money amounts [13, 14].
* **How to use it:**
  1. Locate the decimal point (if not written explicitly, it sits immediately to the right of the units digit) [13].
  2. Move the decimal point two spaces to the left [13].
  3. If moving past the leading digit leaves empty spots, insert placeholder zeros [13].
  4. Always place a zero before the decimal point if there are no whole numbers (e.g., $0.3$, not $.3$) [13].

---

#### Rule 3: Decimal $\to$ Percentage

$$\text{Percentage} = (\text{Decimal Value} \times 100)\%$$ [14]

* **Hot Tip (Shortcut):** Shift the decimal point **two places to the right** and append $\% \text{ symbol}$ [14].
* **What it means:** Express a decimal ratio as a value out of $100$ [14].
* **Why it works:** Multiplying by $100$ shifts place values by $2$ orders of magnitude to match the "per hundred" scale [13, 14].
* **When to use it:** When presenting decimal statistical results as percentages [14].
* **How to use it:**
  1. Shift the decimal point two spaces to the right [14].
  2. Fill trailing empty spots with zeros if needed [14].
  3. Add the percent symbol ($\%$) [14].

---

#### Rule 4: Fraction $\to$ Decimal

$$\text{Decimal} = \text{Numerator} \div \text{Denominator}$$ [14]

* **What it means:** Perform the division implied by the fraction bar [14].
* **Why it works:** The fraction bar $\frac{a}{b}$ is mathematically defined as $a \div b$ [14].
* **When to use it:** When converting fractions into a form suitable for calculators or decimal comparisons [14, 15].
* **How to use it:**
  * **Method A (Unit Fraction Approach):** Determine the decimal value of the unit fraction $\frac{1}{b}$, then multiply by the numerator [14].
  * **Method B (Direct Long Division):** Divide numerator by denominator [14].

---

#### Rule 5: Decimal $\to$ Fraction

$$\text{Fraction} = \frac{\text{Decimal Digits}}{\text{Place Value Power of 10}}$$ [12, 13]

* **What it means:** Write the decimal digits over their place value denominator ($10, 100, 1000$), then simplify [12, 13].
* **Why it works:** Decimal places directly represent tenths, hundredths, thousandths, etc. [12, 13].
* **When to use it:** When converting measured decimals back into fractional parts [12].

---

#### Rule 6: Fraction $\to$ Percentage

$$\text{Percentage} = \left(\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}} \times 100\right)\%$$ [14, 15]

* **How to use it:**
  * **Method A (Via Decimal):** Convert fraction to decimal first (Rule 4), then move decimal point two places to the right (Rule 3) [14].
  * **Method B (Equivalent Fraction Scaling):** Multiply numerator and denominator by a factor that transforms the denominator into $100$ [15].

### 6. Step-by-Step Examples

#### Example 1: Converting $30\%$ to a Fraction and a Decimal (Easy)
*Source: Video Tutorial [12, 13]*

**Problem:** Convert $30\%$ into (a) a simplified fraction and (b) a decimal [12, 13].

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

**(a) Convert $30\%$ to a Fraction:**
1. Write $30\%$ as a fraction out of $100$:
   $$\frac{30}{100}$$ [12]
2. Simplify by dividing numerator and denominator by $10$ (use the zero-canceling shortcut):
   $$\frac{3\cancel{0}}{10\cancel{0}} = \frac{3}{10}$$ [12]

**(b) Convert $30\%$ to a Decimal:**
* **Method 1 (Using Place Value):** The fraction is $\frac{3}{10}$ (three tenths) [12, 13]. Place a $3$ in the tenths place:
  $$0.3$$ [12, 13]
* **Method 2 (Using Decimal Shift Shortcut):** Take the implied decimal point in $30.$ and shift it $2$ places to the left:
  $$30. \longrightarrow 0.30 = 0.3$$ [13]

**Final Answer:** $30\% = \frac{3}{10} = 0.3$ [12, 13]

---

#### Example 2: Calculating $8.25\%$ Sales Tax on a $\$50$ Purchase (Medium)
*Source: Video Tutorial [13, 14]*

**Problem:** Express $8.25\%$ sales tax as a decimal, and compute the tax amount on a $\$50$ shirt [13, 14].

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Convert $8.25\%$ to a Decimal:**
   * Place decimal point at $8.25\%$ [13].
   * Shift decimal point $2$ places to the left [13]:
     * Shift $1$ place left: $0.825$
     * Shift $2$ places left (add placeholder zero): $0.0825$ [13]
   
   $$8.25\% = 0.0825$$ [13]

2. **Compute the Sales Tax on $\$50$:**
   * Multiply the purchase price by the decimal tax rate [13, 14]:
     $$\text{Tax Amount} = \$50 \times 0.0825$$ [14]
   * Calculation:
     $$50 \times 0.0825 = 4.125$$ [14]

3. **Interpret Monetary Result:**
   * $\$4.125$ equals $4$ dollars and $12.5$ cents (rounds to $\$4.13$ in financial transactions) [14].

**Final Answer:** The tax rate as a decimal is $0.0825$, and the sales tax on a $\$50$ shirt is $\$4.125$ (or $\$4.13$) [13, 14].

---

#### Example 3: Converting $0.84$ to a Percentage (Easy)
*Source: Video Tutorial [14]*

**Problem:** Convert the decimal $0.84$ into a percentage [14].

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Apply Rule 3: Shift decimal point $2$ places to the right [14]:
   $$0.84 \longrightarrow 84.$$ [14]
2. Add the percent symbol ($\%$) [14]:
   $$84\%$$ [14]

**Final Answer:** $0.84 = 84\%$ [14]

---

#### Example 4: Converting $\frac{3}{5}$ to a Decimal and Percentage (Medium)
*Source: Video Tutorial [14]*

**Problem:** Convert $\frac{3}{5}$ into a decimal and a percentage [14].

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Find the Decimal Value:**
   * Determine unit fraction value $\frac{1}{5}$ [14]:
     $$\frac{1}{5} = 0.2 \quad (\text{since } 5 \times 0.2 = 1.0)$$ [14]
   * Multiply by numerator $3$ [14]:
     $$\frac{3}{5} = 3 \times 0.2 = 0.6$$ [14]

2. **Convert Decimal $0.6$ to Percentage:**
   * Shift decimal point $2$ places to the right (adding a trailing zero placeholder) [14]:
     $$0.60 \longrightarrow 60\%$$ [14]

**Final Answer:** $\frac{3}{5} = 0.6 = 60\%$ [14]

---

#### Example 5: Ordering Values from Least to Greatest (Challenging)
*Source: Video Tutorial [15, 16]*

**Problem:** Arrange the following three values in order from least to greatest:
$$45\%, \quad \frac{21}{50}, \quad 0.43$$ [15]

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

To compare numbers accurately, convert all values into a common format (such as fractions with denominator $100$ or percentages) [15].

1. **Convert $45\%$:**
   $$45\% = \frac{45}{100} = 45\%$$ [15]

2. **Convert $\frac{21}{50}$ (Scale denominator to $100$):**
   * Multiply both numerator and denominator by $2$ [15]:
     $$\frac{21 \times 2}{50 \times 2} = \frac{42}{100} = 42\%$$ [15]

3. **Convert $0.43$ to Percentage / Fraction over 100:**
   * Shift decimal point $2$ places right [15]:
     $$0.43 = 43\% = \frac{43}{100}$$ [15]

4. **Compare the Values:**
   * Compare numerators ($42 < 43 < 45$) or percentages ($42\% < 43\% < 45\%$) [15, 16]:
     $$\frac{42}{100} < \frac{43}{100} < \frac{45}{100}$$ [15, 16]

5. **Write in Original Forms:**
   $$\frac{21}{50} < 0.43 < 45\%$$ [15, 16]

**Final Answer:** From least to greatest, the correct order is $\frac{21}{50}, 0.43, 45\%$ [15, 16].

### 8. Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

#### Mistake 1: Shifting Decimal Point in the Wrong Direction
* **Incorrect:** Converting $25\%$ to decimal as $2500$ (shifted right instead of left).
* **Why it is wrong:** Percentages represent parts of $100$, so the decimal number must be smaller than the percentage value [12, 13].
* **How to fix:** Remember: **P $\to$ D (Percent to Decimal) = Move Left** (Divide by 100) [13]. **D $\to$ P (Decimal to Percent) = Move Right** (Multiply by 100) [14].

#### Mistake 2: Missing Placeholder Zeros
* **Incorrect:** $6.5\% = 0.65$
* **Why it is wrong:** $0.65 = 65\%$, which is ten times larger than $6.5\%$ [13, 14].
* **How to fix:** Fill every empty jump space with a zero placeholder when moving the decimal point past single digits [13]:
  $$6.5\% \longrightarrow .065 \longrightarrow 0.065$$ [13]

#### Mistake 3: Omitting the Leading Zero
* **Incorrect:** Writing $.3$ instead of $0.3$ [13].
* **Why it is wrong:** In mathematical standards, an isolated decimal point can be easily misread or overlooked [13].
* **How to fix:** Always write a zero before the decimal point when there is no whole number ($0.3$) [13].

#### Mistake 4: Scaling Only the Denominator
* **Incorrect:** Scaling $\frac{21}{50}$ to $\frac{21}{100}$ [15].
* **Why it is wrong:** Changing only the denominator alters the value of the fraction [15].
* **How to fix:** Always multiply both top and bottom by the exact same multiplier [15]:
  $$\frac{21 \times 2}{50 \times 2} = \frac{42}{100}$$ [15]

### 10. Solutions

#### Solution to Problem 1 (Easy)
* **(a) Fraction:** $25\% = \frac{25}{100}$. Divide numerator and denominator by $25$:
  $$\frac{25 \div 25}{100 \div 25} = \frac{1}{4}$$
* **(b) Decimal:** Shift decimal point $2$ places left:
  $$25.\% \longrightarrow 0.25$$

#### Solution to Problem 2 (Easy)
* Shift decimal point $2$ places right:
  $$0.75 \longrightarrow 75\%$$

#### Solution to Problem 3 (Easy)
* **Decimal:** $7$ in tenths place $= 0.7$
* **Percentage:** Shift decimal $2$ places right $= 70\%$

#### Solution to Problem 4 (Medium)
* **Decimal:** Shift decimal point $2$ places left, adding placeholder zero:
  $$6.5\% \longrightarrow 0.065$$
* **Fraction:** $0.065 = \frac{65}{1000}$. Divide numerator and denominator by $5$:
  $$\frac{65 \div 5}{1000 \div 5} = \frac{13}{200}$$

#### Solution to Problem 5 (Medium)
* **Method A (Scaling Denominator to 100):**
  $$\frac{9 \times 5}{20 \times 5} = \frac{45}{100} = 45\%$$
* **Method B (Decimal Conversion First):**
  $$9 \div 20 = 0.45 \longrightarrow 0.45 \times 100 = 45\%$$

#### Solution to Problem 6 (Medium)
1. Convert tip rate $15\%$ to decimal:
   $$15\% = 0.15$$
2. Calculate tip amount:
   $$\text{Tip} = \$32.00 \times 0.15 = \$4.80$$

#### Solution to Problem 7 (Challenging)
Convert all values to percentages to compare:
1. $0.62 = 62\%$
2. $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{3 \times 20}{5 \times 20} = \frac{60}{100} = 60\%$
3. $61.5\% = 61.5\%$
4. $\frac{5}{8} = 5 \div 8 = 0.625 = 62.5\%$

Compare percentage values:
$$60\% < 61.5\% < 62\% < 62.5\%$$

Write in original forms:
$$\frac{3}{5} < 61.5\% < 0.62 < \frac{5}{8}$$

#### Solution to Problem 8 (Challenging)
* **(a) Decimal and Percentage:**
  $$\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8 = 0.375$$
  $$0.375 \times 100 = 37.5\%$$
* **(b) Dollar Amount Saved and Final Price:**
  $$\text{Discount Amount} = \$120.00 \times 0.375 = \$45.00$$
  $$\text{Final Price} = \$120.00 - \$45.00 = \$75.00$$

### 11. Summary

* Fractions, decimals, and percentages express the same underlying values in different forms [12, 15].
* Percentages are ratios based on $100$ [12].
* Moving between percentages and decimals simply requires shifting the decimal point two places [13, 14]:
  * **Left** when dividing by $100$ ($\% \to \text{Decimal}$) [13]
  * **Right** when multiplying by $100$ ($\text{Decimal} \to \%$) [14]
* Fractions convert to decimals through division ($\text{Numerator} \div \text{Denominator}$) [14].

---

### 12. Key Things to Remember

* $\mathbf{P\% = \frac{P}{100}}$ [12]
* **Zero-Canceling Shortcut:** $\frac{30}{100} = \frac{3}{10}$ [12]
* **Percent to Decimal:** Shift decimal point **2 places left** [13]
* **Decimal to Percent:** Shift decimal point **2 places right** [14]
* **Always put a leading zero before a decimal point** ($0.3$, not $.3$) [13]
* **Insert placeholder zeros** when jumping past empty place values ($8.25\% = 0.0825$) [13]
* **To compare different formats:** Convert all values into a single common format (such as percentages or fractions over 100) first [15].
