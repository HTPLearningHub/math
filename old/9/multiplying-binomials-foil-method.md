# Multiplying Binomials by the FOIL Method

#### 1. Introduction
Multiplying polynomials is an essential skill in algebra [12]. Before working with long algebraic expressions, it is important to master multiplying smaller expressions such as monomials and binomials [12]. A monomial is an expression with a single term (such as $2x$ or $3$), while a binomial is an expression containing exactly two terms (such as $x + 3$ or $x - 2$) [12]. 

When multiplying two binomials together, using the basic distributive property directly can feel unclear or easy to mix up [12]. To solve this, we use a structured step-by-step technique called the **FOIL method** [13]. The FOIL method ensures that every term in the first binomial is distributed across every term in the second binomial without skipping any calculations [12, 13]. Learning this method provides a solid foundation for solving quadratic equations, simplifying complex algebraic expressions, and succeeding in higher-level mathematics [1, 12].

---

#### 2. Prerequisite Knowledge
To understand how to multiply binomials, you need to be familiar with a few fundamental algebraic operations:

1. **Multiplying Monomials (Single Terms)** [12]:
   - When multiplying a constant number by a variable term:
     $$2x \times 3 = 6x$$
     *Explanation*: Just like $2 \text{ apples} \times 3 = 6 \text{ apples}$, multiplying $2x$ by $3$ triples the count of $x$ [12].
   - When multiplying two terms that both contain variables:
     $$2x \times 3x = 6x^2$$
     *Explanation*: Multiply the coefficients (the numbers) together ($2 \times 3 = 6$) and multiply the variables together ($x \times x = x^2$) [12].

2. **Multiplying a Monomial by a Binomial (Distributive Property)** [12]:
   - When multiplying a single term by a two-term expression in parentheses, distribute the single term to each term inside [12]:
     $$2x(x - 4) = (2x \cdot x) - (2x \cdot 4) = 2x^2 - 8x$$

3. **Combining Like Terms** [13, 15]:
   - Like terms are terms that have the exact same variable parts raised to the same power [13]. Only coefficients of like terms can be added or subtracted [13].
   - For example: $-2x + 3x = 1x = x$, but $x^2$ and $x$ cannot be combined because their exponents are different [13, 15].

---

#### 3. Important Definitions
- **Polynomial**: An algebraic expression made up of terms added or subtracted together [12].
- **Monomial**: A polynomial consisting of exactly one term (e.g., $2x$, $5$, $x^2$) [12].
- **Binomial**: A polynomial consisting of exactly two terms connected by a plus or minus sign (e.g., $x + 3$, $2x - 4$) [12].
- **Trinomial**: A polynomial consisting of exactly three terms (e.g., $3x^2 - 5x + 2$) [15].
- **Distributive Property**: A mathematical rule stating that multiplying a sum by a number gives the same result as multiplying each addend individually and then adding the products: $a(b + c) = ab + ac$ [12, 13].
- **FOIL Method**: A memory aid (acronym) for multiplying two binomials by applying the distributive property twice [12, 13].
- **Like Terms**: Terms with identical variables and identical exponents (e.g., $3x$ and $-2x$) [13].

---

#### 4. Main Concepts

##### Why the FOIL Method Works
When multiplying two binomials, such as $(x + 3)(x - 2)$, each term in the first binomial must be multiplied by each term in the second binomial [12, 13]. This is equivalent to using the distributive property twice [12, 13]:
1. Distribute $x$ across $(x - 2) \rightarrow x(x - 2) = x^2 - 2x$ [12, 13].
2. Distribute $+3$ across $(x - 2) \rightarrow 3(x - 2) = 3x - 6$ [12, 13].
3. Add the partial results together $\rightarrow x^2 - 2x + 3x - 6 = x^2 + x - 6$ [13].

The **FOIL** acronym simply organizes this process into four clear steps so that you perform the multiplications in the exact same order every single time [13]:

- **F**irst: Multiply the first terms in each binomial [13].
- **O**uter: Multiply the outer (outermost) terms of the two binomials [13].
- **I**nner: Multiply the inner (innermost) terms of the two binomials [13].
- **L**ast: Multiply the last terms in each binomial [13].

```
       First (F)
    ┌──────────────┐
    │              │
 (x + 3)        (x - 2)
    │   │      │   │
    │   └──────┘   │
    │    Inner (I) │
    └──────────────┘
        Outer (O)
```

##### Extending Beyond Binomials
While FOIL specifically applies to two binomials (yielding 4 multiplication operations), the core concept—distributing every term of the first polynomial across every term of the second—applies to larger expressions [15, 16]:
- **Binomial $\times$ Trinomial**: Distribute the 2 terms of the binomial across the 3 terms of the trinomial, producing $2 \times 3 = 6$ terms before combining like terms [15].
- **Trinomial $\times$ Trinomial**: Distribute the 3 terms across 3 terms, producing $3 \times 3 = 9$ terms before combining like terms [15, 16].

---

#### 5. Formulas and Rules

##### The FOIL Formula
For any two binomials $(a + b)$ and $(c + d)$:

$$(a + b)(c + d) = \underbrace{ac}_{\text{First}} + \underbrace{ad}_{\text{Outer}} + \underbrace{bc}_{\text{Inner}} + \underbrace{bd}_{\text{Last}}$$ [13]

##### Rules for Handling Negative Signs
A common point of confusion is how to handle minus signs between terms in a binomial [13, 14]. 
- Do not view the binomial as numbers separated by separate subtraction operations [13, 14].
- Instead, view each binomial as a **list of two terms, where each term carries its own positive or negative sign** [13, 14].
- For example, in $(x - 2)$, the terms are $x$ and $-2$ [13, 14].
- When performing multiplication, apply standard sign rules:
  - $\text{Positive} \times \text{Positive} = \text{Positive}$ [14]
  - $\text{Positive} \times \text{Negative} = \text{Negative}$ [14]
  - $\text{Negative} \times \text{Negative} = \text{Positive}$ [14]
- Write out each result with its sign ($+$ or $-$) directly into a running line of terms [14].

#### 6. Step-by-Step Examples

##### Example 1 (Easy / Standard Binomials) [12, 13]
Multiply the binomials: $(x + 3)(x - 2)$

**Step-by-step solution**:
1. **F (First)**: Multiply the first terms in each set of parentheses [13]:
   $$x \cdot x = x^2$$
2. **O (Outer)**: Multiply the outermost terms ($x$ and $-2$) [13, 14]:
   $$x \cdot (-2) = -2x$$
3. **I (Inner)**: Multiply the innermost terms ($3$ and $x$) [13]:
   $$3 \cdot x = 3x$$
4. **L (Last)**: Multiply the last terms in each set of parentheses ($3$ and $-2$) [13, 14]:
   $$3 \cdot (-2) = -6$$
5. **Write down the initial expression** [13]:
   $$x^2 - 2x + 3x - 6$$
6. **Combine like terms** ($-2x + 3x = 1x = x$) [13]:
   $$\mathbf{x^2 + x - 6}$$

---

##### Example 2 (Medium / Variable Exponents and Negative Coefficients) [14]
Multiply the binomials: $(x^2 + 4)(-2x - 1)$

**Step-by-step solution**:
1. **F (First)**: Multiply $x^2$ by $-2x$ [14]:
   $$x^2 \cdot (-2x) = -2x^3$$
2. **O (Outer)**: Multiply $x^2$ by $-1$ [14]:
   $$x^2 \cdot (-1) = -x^2$$
3. **I (Inner)**: Multiply $4$ by $-2x$ [14]:
   $$4 \cdot (-2x) = -8x$$
4. **L (Last)**: Multiply $4$ by $-1$ [14]:
   $$4 \cdot (-1) = -4$$
5. **Write down the expression** [14]:
   $$-2x^3 - x^2 - 8x - 4$$
6. **Check for like terms**: Inspect the powers of $x$ ($x^3$, $x^2$, $x^1$, constant) [14, 15]. There are no like terms to combine [14, 15].
   $$\mathbf{-2x^3 - x^2 - 8x - 4}$$

---

##### Example 3 (Challenging / Binomial $\times$ Trinomial with Two Variables) [15]
Multiply the binomial by the trinomial: $(x + 2y)(3x - y - 4)$

**Step-by-step solution**:
Since the second factor is a trinomial, we distribute each term of the binomial across all three terms of the trinomial [15].

1. **Distribute the first term ($x$) across $(3x - y - 4)$** [15]:
   - $x \cdot 3x = 3x^2$ [15]
   - $x \cdot (-y) = -xy$ [15]
   - $x \cdot (-4) = -4x$ [15]
   - Partial product 1: $3x^2 - xy - 4x$ [15]

2. **Distribute the second term ($2y$) across $(3x - y - 4)$** [15]:
   - $2y \cdot 3x = 6xy$ [15]
   - $2y \cdot (-y) = -2y^2$ [15]
   - $2y \cdot (-4) = -8y$ [15]
   - Partial product 2: $6xy - 2y^2 - 8y$ [15]

3. **Combine both partial products into one list** [15]:
   $$3x^2 - xy - 4x + 6xy - 2y^2 - 8y$$

4. **Identify and combine like terms** [15]:
   - The like terms are $-xy$ and $6xy$ [15].
   - $-1xy + 6xy = 5xy$ [15].

5. **Arrange by standard convention** (squared terms first, then combined variable terms, then linear terms) [15]:
   $$\mathbf{3x^2 - 2y^2 + 5xy - 4x - 8y}$$

#### 8. Common Mistakes

##### Mistake 1: Confusing positive and negative signs [13, 14]
- **Incorrect approach**: Writing $(x + 3)(x - 2)$ as $x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6$ because of ignoring minus signs [13, 14].
- **Why it is wrong**: The term is $-2$, not $+2$ [13, 14]. $x \cdot (-2) = -2x$ and $3 \cdot (-2) = -6$ [13].
- **How to avoid**: Always treat each term as possessing the sign directly in front of it ($+$ or $-$) [14].

##### Mistake 2: Forgetting the middle terms (Outer and Inner) [12, 13]
- **Incorrect approach**: $(x + 3)(x - 2) = x^2 - 6$ [12, 13].
- **Why it is wrong**: The student only multiplied the First terms ($x \cdot x$) and the Last terms ($3 \cdot -2$), completely forgetting the Outer and Inner products [12, 13].
- **How to avoid**: Always spell out **F-O-I-L** to guarantee you calculate all 4 terms [13].

##### Mistake 3: Stopping before combining like terms [13, 15]
- **Incorrect stopping point**: Leaving the final answer as $x^2 - 2x + 3x - 6$ [13].
- **Why it is incomplete**: $-2x$ and $3x$ are like terms and must be simplified [13].
- **How to avoid**: Always check your 4 resulting terms to see if any contain identical variable powers that can be merged [13, 15].

#### 10. Solutions

##### Solution to Problem 1: $(x + 4)(x + 5)$ [8, 10]
- **F**: $x \cdot x = x^2$
- **O**: $x \cdot 5 = 5x$
- **I**: $4 \cdot x = 4x$
- **L**: $4 \cdot 5 = 20$
- **Combine like terms**: $5x + 4x = 9x$
- **Final Answer**: $\mathbf{x^2 + 9x + 20}$

##### Solution to Problem 2: $(x + 2)(x - 5)$ [8, 10]
- **F**: $x \cdot x = x^2$
- **O**: $x \cdot (-5) = -5x$
- **I**: $2 \cdot x = 2x$
- **L**: $2 \cdot (-5) = -10$
- **Combine like terms**: $-5x + 2x = -3x$
- **Final Answer**: $\mathbf{x^2 - 3x - 10}$

##### Solution to Problem 3: $(2x - 3)(3x + 4)$ [8, 10]
- **F**: $2x \cdot 3x = 6x^2$
- **O**: $2x \cdot 4 = 8x$
- **I**: $-3 \cdot 3x = -9x$
- **L**: $-3 \cdot 4 = -12$
- **Combine like terms**: $8x - 9x = -x$
- **Final Answer**: $\mathbf{6x^2 - x - 12}$

##### Solution to Problem 4: $(x^2 + 3)(-3x - 2)$ [8, 10, 14]
- **F**: $x^2 \cdot (-3x) = -3x^3$
- **O**: $x^2 \cdot (-2) = -2x^2$
- **I**: $3 \cdot (-3x) = -9x$
- **L**: $3 \cdot (-2) = -6$
- **Check like terms**: Powers are $x^3, x^2, x^1$, and constant—no like terms to combine [14].
- **Final Answer**: $\mathbf{-3x^3 - 2x^2 - 9x - 6}$

##### Solution to Problem 5: $(2x + y)(x - 3y + 2)$ [8, 10, 15]
- **Distribute $2x$**: 
  - $2x \cdot x = 2x^2$
  - $2x \cdot (-3y) = -6xy$
  - $2x \cdot 2 = 4x$
- **Distribute $y$**: 
  - $y \cdot x = xy$
  - $y \cdot (-3y) = -3y^2$
  - $y \cdot 2 = 2y$
- **Combine expression**: $2x^2 - 6xy + 4x + xy - 3y^2 + 2y$
- **Combine like terms**: $-6xy + xy = -5xy$ [15]
- **Final Answer**: $\mathbf{2x^2 - 3y^2 - 5xy + 4x + 2y}$

#### 11. Summary
The FOIL method is a straightforward mnemonic technique designed to help students multiply two binomials accurately using double distribution [12, 13]. By systematically calculating the **First**, **Outer**, **Inner**, and **Last** terms, you ensure that every term in one binomial is multiplied by every term in the other [13]. Always remember to attach signs directly to terms, watch out for double negatives, and combine like terms at the end to state your answer in standard form [13, 14, 15].

---

#### 12. Key Things to Remember
- **FOIL Definition**: $\text{First}, \text{Outer}, \text{Inner}, \text{Last}$ [13].
- **Formula**: $(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd$ [13].
- **Sign Rule**: Treat each term with its preceding sign ($+$ or $-$) attached [14].
- **Variables Rule**: When multiplying identical variables, add exponents ($x \cdot x = x^2$) [12].
- **Final Step**: Always inspect for and combine like terms ($O + I$) [13].
- **Beyond FOIL**: For expressions with more terms, distribute each term systematically [15].
